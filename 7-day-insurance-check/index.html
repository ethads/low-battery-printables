There is a drawer, or a folder, or an email search that you have been avoiding. Inside is the paperwork for the insurance you pay for. You are not totally sure what is in it. You are not sure what you would be covered for if something happened. You do not know when it renews, or who to ring.

You are not alone. Most people carry insurance on trust. They pay each month, hope for the best, and avoid looking in case they find a gap they cannot afford to fix. The longer it goes, the heavier it gets.

Why it is hard to start

Insurance papers are written in language that seems designed to make your eyes slide off the page. The documents are long. The terms are unfamiliar. And checking feels risky, because what if you find out you are not covered? Avoiding the question feels easier than answering it.

But finding out is almost always less bad than not knowing. You can only make a choice about something you can see.

The week, one job a day

Twenty minutes at most. You are not buying, cancelling or comparing anything unless you choose to. You are only finding out.

  1. Monday: list what you have. Search your email and bank statements for insurance, policy, premium and cover. Write each one down with the company and the cost.
  2. Tuesday: find the paperwork. Locate the policy schedule or certificate for each. Save them in one folder.
  3. Wednesday: check what is covered. Find the summary page. Write one line: what it covers, and the biggest thing it does not.
  4. Thursday: find the excess. The amount you pay yourself when you claim. Write it next to each policy.
  5. Friday: check the renewal. Put each date in your phone with an alarm four weeks before.
  6. Saturday: save the claim numbers. Put each insurer's claims phone number in your contacts, with the policy number in the notes.
  7. Sunday: write the one page. Every policy, date, excess and number on a single sheet.

The words to say

When you cannot find a document, ask for it:

"Hi, can you please email me a copy of my policy documents and the current schedule? The policy number is [number]."

When a renewal price has gone up:

"Hi, my policy [number] is due to renew on [date] and I noticed the price has gone up. Is there a better price available for me, or any discount you can apply?"

When you want to check cover:

"Hi, I'm looking at my policy and I'd like to check what is and isn't covered for [the thing]. Could you explain it in plain terms?"

A word about advice

This week is about information, not decisions. A guide like this cannot tell you what to buy, what to cancel or whether your cover is enough. What it can do is give you the facts in one place, so that if you speak to a qualified adviser or an official insurance information service in your country, you already know what you have and what to ask.

What counts as done

Done on Monday is a list. Done on Thursday is a number next to every policy. By Sunday, done is a single page in your own handwriting that tells you who you are insured with, what it covers, what you would pay, when it renews and who to ring. Four weeks before each renewal, spend five minutes with the letter, and ring if the price has risen. Check again after any big change, such as a move, a new car or a baby.
