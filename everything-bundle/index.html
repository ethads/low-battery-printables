You've read a few of the listings and had the same thought each time: that one's me too. The scary mail, the inbox, the car, the burnout week. It's not that you have eight separate problems. It's that admin is one problem with eight faces, and a brain that struggles with starting struggles with all of them in the same way.

So before the bundle, it's worth being clear on what a system that actually works for that brain looks like. Not because you need convincing, but because knowing the shape of the thing makes it far more likely you'll use it.

Why this is harder than it looks

Most admin advice assumes the hard part is knowing what to do. For ADHD and low-energy brains the hard part is almost never that. You know you should open the mail. The hard parts are starting, choosing what today's task is, knowing when you're allowed to stop, and finding the words for the phone call. A system that doesn't address those four things is a list of good intentions.

The other failure is that systems get too big. An elaborate planner works for exactly one week and then becomes one more thing you're behind on.

What the system looks like

Four pieces, and they're all small.

  • One-page sheets for the acute stuff. The pile of mail, the overdue bill, the thing you need to say on the phone. One page, ten minutes, one thing done. These are for the day you're in trouble, not for building habits.
  • Seven-day guides for the areas that need rebuilding. Money, home, health, inbox, phone, car, parenting, freelancing, emergencies, burnout. One job a day, twenty minutes maximum, in a fixed order so you never decide what today is. Miss a day and do it the next one.
  • Scripts for every conversation. Written out, read aloud, no composing under pressure. Asking for a payment plan, switching insurer, telling one person you're not okay, chasing an invoice.
  • A definition of done for every task. The single thing that means you're allowed to stop. Without it, a task never ends and so never starts.

That's the whole system. Everything is printable, A4, and reads fine on a phone. You use what the week needs and ignore the rest.

How you'd actually use it

Not all at once. The way in is one sheet or one guide, picked by whatever's loudest right now. A sheet when something is on fire. A guide when an area keeps generating fires. Over a few months the guides stop the fires, so the sheets get used less, and the pile of unread admin you've carried for years quietly gets smaller.

The kit matters because the loudest thing changes. This month it's the car. Next month it's a school form or a client who hasn't paid. Having the right page already on the shelf is what turns a bad week into a twenty-minute job.

The words to say

Every guide and sheet carries its own scripts, but the thing they have in common is this shape:

> "Hi, I'm calling about [thing]. I've got behind and I'd like to sort it out. What are my options?"

Plain, no apology, no backstory. Most of the fear around admin is fear of this sentence, and it turns out to be the easiest part.

What counts as done

Done isn't a tidy life. Done is having a page for the thing that's wrong today, and knowing that when you've done its one job you're allowed to stop. That's a smaller ambition than most systems promise, which is exactly why it keeps working.
