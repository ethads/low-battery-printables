How to organise the admin of moving out of a rental in a week without losing your deposit

Seven days of move-out admin for renters: confirm dates, photograph every room, close the bills, redirect post and plan the handover. Twenty minutes a day.

You have a date. In a few weeks, or a few days, you will not live here any more. The thought is exciting and horrible at once. There are boxes to pack, and behind the boxes there is a second, quieter list: the deposit, the keys, the bills, the post, the cleaning, the thousand tiny things that will go wrong if you forget them.

Moving is one of the most demanding things a low-energy brain gets asked to do. It is a pile of different jobs, each with its own deadline, and all of them have to happen while you are also physically exhausted.

Why the admin is the part that bites

The packing is visible. You can see the boxes filling up. The admin is invisible, and nothing nags you about it until it goes wrong. A bill keeps running after you leave. Post goes to the next tenant. A scuff you could have photographed becomes an argument about the deposit.

The fix is to deal with the invisible jobs in small pieces, in an order that protects you first. That way, even if the packing goes badly, the things that cost real money are covered.

The week, one job a day

Twenty minutes at most. If your move date is close, do the most urgent day first.

  1. Monday: confirm the dates. Find the notice period and the end date in your agreement. Write down the move-out date and handover date, and send a short message to confirm them in writing.
  2. Tuesday: photograph every room. Wide shots and close-ups of anything worn or damaged, saved in one folder, named by room.
  3. Wednesday: close the bills. Power, gas, water, internet and subscriptions. Give each your move-out date and ask for a final reading.
  4. Thursday: update the address. Set up post redirection and tell your bank, doctor, employer and insurers.
  5. Friday: sort the small repairs. Fix what takes ten minutes. Report the rest in writing.
  6. Saturday: plan the handover. Inspection time, key plan, cleaning plan and how the deposit comes back.
  7. Sunday: write the one page. Dates, contacts, bills and keys on one sheet, plus a reminder to check the deposit.

The words to say

Confirming your dates:

"Hi, I'm writing to confirm I'll be moving out of [address] on [date]. Could you confirm in writing that this is correct, and let me know how you'd like the keys returned and when the final inspection will be?"

Closing a bill:

"Hi, I'm moving out of [address] on [date]. I'd like to close my account, or transfer it to my new address, from that day. Can you take a final meter reading on [date] and send me the final bill by email?"

Asking about the deposit:

"Hi, could you confirm how and when the deposit will be returned? If there are any deductions, please send them in writing."

Keep everything in writing

Dates, replies and photos protect you. Send messages rather than relying on a phone call, and screenshot anything you receive. If you speak on the phone, send a short message afterwards saying what you agreed.

Check your own rules

Rules about notice, cleaning and deposits differ from place to place and from agreement to agreement. Read your own tenancy agreement and your local rules. If you are unsure whether your notice is valid or a deduction is fair, a free tenant advice service in your country can tell you where you stand.

What counts as done

Done on Monday is two dates and a message sent. Done on Tuesday is a folder of dated photos. By Sunday, done is a single page that holds the dates, who to tell, which bills are closed and the keys plan. After handover, check the deposit reminder. The rest is boxes.
