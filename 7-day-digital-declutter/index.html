How to clear out your phone, inbox and files in a week without losing anything important

Seven days to lighten a full phone and a cluttered laptop: back up first, delete ten apps, quiet the alerts, clear screenshots and tidy the inbox.

Your phone says storage is almost full. Your inbox shows a number in five figures. The desktop on your laptop is a jumble of files with names like Untitled and final_final2. None of it is dangerous, but all of it is heavy, and every time you glance at it you feel a small pang of failure.

The problem is that digital clutter has no edges. A physical drawer eventually fills up and forces you to deal with it. A phone or a cloud account quietly keeps growing. There is no moment where it demands action, and no point at which you can say you have finished.

Why it keeps getting worse

Clearing digital clutter feels risky. What if you delete the one photo you needed? What if that email was important? So you leave everything, which is the safest choice in the short term and the most expensive one in the long term. Each piece of clutter is also a tiny decision, and decisions are what a tired brain avoids most.

The way through is an order that removes the fear first. You protect what matters, then delete only what is obviously junk, then quiet what keeps adding to the pile.

The week, one job a day

Twenty minutes at most each day. Miss one and do it the next day.

  1. Monday: back up what matters. Make sure your phone's backup is on and finished, and your photos are saved somewhere else. Nothing gets deleted until this is done.
  2. Tuesday: delete ten apps. The ones you have not opened in months. Check for subscriptions first, because deleting an app does not cancel its payments.
  3. Wednesday: quiet the notifications. Turn off everything except calls, people and the bank.
  4. Thursday: clear the photos. Screenshots, duplicates, blurry shots. Leave the family photos alone.
  5. Friday: trim the inbox. Unsubscribe from the five senders who email you most and archive anything older than a year.
  6. Saturday: tidy downloads and desktop. Delete old installers, put anything you might need into one folder called KEEP, and clear the desktop.
  7. Sunday: check the cloud and write the page. Look at the biggest files, delete duplicates, and write a ten-minute monthly routine.

Questions that make deleting easy

You do not need a long think for each item. Use one question and stick to it. For apps: "Have I opened this in the last three months?" For photos: "Would I be sad if this was gone?" For files: "If I am not sure, it goes in KEEP." Five seconds each is enough.

If you cannot find the unsubscribe link

Use this:

"Please remove me from your mailing list. I no longer wish to receive these emails."

Do not click unsubscribe on messages from senders you do not recognise. Mark those as spam.

A note on safety

Archive is not delete. Archived emails are still searchable. Deleted photos usually sit in a recently deleted folder for around a month. Empty the bin or trash only when you are sure. Never delete anything you have not backed up, and do not delete what you cannot replace.

What counts as done

Done on Monday is a backup that finished today. Done on Thursday is a smaller storage bar. By Sunday, done is a lighter phone, a calmer inbox, a clearer desktop and a page with one rule on it: ten minutes on the first of every month, to clear screenshots, empty downloads and check the backup. You are not aiming for tidy. You are aiming for light enough that nothing weighs on you.
