What to sort out first after a breakup when you can barely function

A seven-day plan for the practical side of a breakup: list what is shared, secure accounts, check the money, sort belongings and tell the right people.

A breakup is two things happening at once. The first is the grief, which takes as long as it takes and has no schedule. The second is the admin, which has plenty of schedule and does not care how you feel. Rent is still due. The streaming account is still in two names. Your things are in a flat you no longer live in, and your phone still shows where they are.

If you are in the middle of this, you are not failing by finding the practical part hard. It is a lot, and it lands at the time you have least to give.

Start with a list, not a decision

The most useful thing you can do first is also the smallest: write down what is shared. Draw four columns: money, home, bills and accounts, belongings. List everything joint or tied together. Next to each, write whose name it is in and who pays. Star the three that cause you the most stress.

A page of facts is much smaller than the cloud of worry it replaces. It also stops you doing anything in a hurry that you may regret. Do not delete, take or cancel anything shared until you have written it down.

The week, one job a day

Twenty minutes each, at most. Feelings will turn up in the gaps. Let them, then do the job. If you miss a day, do it the next. Some jobs can wait for a calmer week.

  1. List what is shared. As above.
  2. Secure your own accounts. Change your email password first, since it resets everything else, and turn on two-step sign-in. Check which devices are signed in, and turn off location sharing and shared albums you no longer want. This is basic hygiene, not hostility.
  3. Look at the money. Write down balances and regular payments on every joint account and keep a record. Do not close or move large amounts yet.
  4. The home. Find the lease or mortgage paperwork. Whose names, what notice terms, what each person pays. Decide your short-term option, even if it changes.
  5. Belongings. Two lists: what of yours is there and what of theirs is with you. Secure your own essential documents first.
  6. Tell the people who need to know. A short list, and short messages.
  7. Plan the next few weeks. Three dated jobs and one thing just for you.

Words that keep things calm

For arranging belongings: "Hi [name], I'd like to sort out collecting my things and returning yours. Would [day and time] work for you? I'm happy to do it with a friend, or to arrange for a courier. Please let me know what suits you best."

For the people who need to know: "Hi [name], I wanted you to hear this from me. [Name] and I have split up. I'm okay, and I'd appreciate some space for now."

To your bank: "I have a joint account and my circumstances have changed. Could you tell me what options there are for separating it, and what happens to shared payments and any overdraft?"

Keep every message short and calm. You may need to refer back to them later, so write only what you would be comfortable seeing read out.

Where this needs a professional

Shared property, a lease, debt, children and anything legal depends heavily on where you live and what you have signed. Please get advice from a qualified professional or a free local advice service before you close accounts, sign anything, give notice or move large amounts of money. A calm week of thinking rarely costs more than a rushed decision.

If you do not feel safe

If you feel unsafe at any point, put that ahead of everything on this list. Contact local emergency services or a domestic violence helpline. Change passwords from a device the other person cannot reach, and ask a friend to be with you for any handover of belongings.

What counts as done

Each day has one thing that counts. A single page of what is shared. Your email password changed. A written snapshot of the balances. The lease located. Your documents secure and one message sent. The practical people told. Three dated jobs in the calendar.

If you only manage Day 2, that is the day that matters most, because your own accounts are the part nobody else can protect for you.

Be kind to yourself in the gaps

A weekly twenty-minute slot for admin, a meal, one friend who knows what is going on. If you feel low for long stretches, talk to your doctor or a counselling service. Support is part of looking after yourself.
