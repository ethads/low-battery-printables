How to tackle a dish pile when you can't face the sink

An eight-minute way to deal with a pile of dishes in four heaps, with soaking allowed and a three-things version for when you have nothing left.

The sink is full. There's a plate you used on Tuesday, a pan that has been soaking so long the water has turned into something else, and the cups have migrated to every surface. You want to do something about it. You've wanted to for days. But every time you walk past, the pile looks like a mountain, and mountains are for people with more energy.

If this is you, nothing is wrong with your character. Washing up is deceptively demanding. It's physical, it's wet, it needs you to stand up, and it needs a dozen small decisions about order and soap and space. If you're tired, depressed or burned out, that's a lot to ask. The pile grows, and a bigger pile makes the first step feel bigger.

Rules for today

You're clearing, not cleaning. A clear sink with a couple of pans soaking is a win. Hot water, a dab of soap, rinse and stack. Nobody is checking your results.

Soaking is allowed. If something is burnt on, fill it with hot water and soap and walk away. It will be easier tomorrow, and that's not procrastinating, that's what soaking is for.

Sort into four heaps (two minutes)

Before you turn the tap on, look at what you've got and sort it into four rough groups:

  • Rubbish. Tins, wrappers, takeaway tubs, anything that doesn't belong in the sink at all. Straight into the bin.
  • Cups and glasses. These go first. They take thirty seconds each and make the biggest visible difference.
  • Plates and cutlery. Rinse off the worst, then stack beside the sink.
  • Pans, soaking. Fill with hot water and a squirt of soap. Leave them.

Count how many of each, if it helps. Counting makes the pile finite, and finite things feel doable.

The wash order (six minutes)

Run the water hot. Do the cups and glasses first, then cutlery, then plates and bowls, then any tupperware and chopping boards. Leave the pans soaking. Put each thing on the rack as you go. Drying with a towel is optional. Air drying is perfectly good.

Finish by wiping the drainer and pulling the plug. A clear sink is the finish line.

If you have a dishwasher

Load from the back, big things on the bottom, glasses on top. You don't need to pre-rinse beyond scraping the worst of the food into the bin. Run it when it's full, or run it tonight so you wake up to empty counters.

If you've got almost nothing left

Here's the small version of the same job. Wash one plate, one cup, one fork. That's three things. It's a real thing, and it lets you have a bowl of something tonight.

Say it to yourself if it helps: "One plate. One cup. One fork. I'm washing three things."

For a few days, paper plates and a few reusable bowls are a legitimate choice. It's not cheating. It's making sure you can eat. You can go back to the dishes when you have more to give.

What counts as done

Done is a sink with no dishes in it, or at least with the cups and plates washed and the pans soaking. It's fine if the pans wait until tomorrow morning, when the kettle's boiling and you can scrub them while you wait. It's fine if the counters still have things on them. It's even fine if you only washed three things, because three is more than none, and tomorrow's pile starts smaller.

If you want one habit to take from this: wash the cup you used before you go to bed. One cup is the difference between a bad morning and an easier one.
