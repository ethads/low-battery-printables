How to deal with the paperwork pile when you can't decide where it goes

There is a pile. It might be on the kitchen bench, in a drawer, or in a bag-for-life behind the door. It has letters from the bank, something from the council, a warranty, a payslip from 2023 and a form you were meant to return. You add to it. You never take from it. You know roughly what's in there and the thought of going through it makes you tired.

This isn't a tidiness problem. A pile forms because every piece of paper asks you a question: where does this go? If there's no ready answer, the paper waits. Multiply by a year and you've got the pile. Decide the answers once, in advance, and the pile stops forming.

Why this is harder than it looks

Most filing systems fail in the labelling. People buy a box of folders, write "Finance" on one and "House" on another, then stall on the third because they can't decide whether the car insurance is "Car" or "Insurance". The system that asks you to design categories is a system that never gets built.

The other failure is maintenance. A beautiful system with no habit attached is a pile with extra steps.

The week, one job a day

Nothing takes more than twenty minutes. Miss a day and you do it the next one; the week can be nine days long.

  1. Name the seven folders. Seven, no more, with the names already chosen so you don't have to invent them. Buy or find seven folders and write the names on.
  2. Triage the pile. Three piles: keep, shred, action. Don't read anything properly. If it's a decision, it's action. If it's old and useless, it's shred. If it's a record, it's keep.
  3. File the keeps. Each keep goes into one of the seven folders. Because there are only seven, every piece has an obvious home within a second or two.
  4. Scan the essentials. Photograph or scan the handful of documents that would be a disaster to lose: ID, certificates, the lease or deed, insurance policies.
  5. The digital twin. Make seven folders on your computer or cloud drive with the same seven names. Scans and emailed PDFs go in the matching one.
  6. The ACTION tray. One tray or folder for anything that needs you to do something. It's the only place unfinished paper is allowed to live.
  7. The Sunday ten. Ten minutes every Sunday. Empty the ACTION tray, file anything new, shred what's done. That's the whole habit.

The words to say

This one is mostly between you and the paper, but there's one message worth having ready. If a company keeps posting you statements you'd rather receive by email:

"Hi, could you switch my account to paperless statements and send them to this email address? Thanks."

And one line to say to yourself when a piece of paper arrives and you're tempted to put it down anywhere: "Which of the seven?" If the answer is "none", it's shred or action.

What counts as done

Done is: seven named folders exist, the pile is gone into keep, shred or action, the keeps are filed, the essentials are scanned, the digital folders match, the ACTION tray exists, and you've done one Sunday ten. Not a label maker in sight. Not a colour-coded anything. Just a place for everything and a ten-minute habit that keeps it that way.
