How to book the doctor's appointment when you can't face the phone call

A five-minute way to make the doctor's appointment call you keep putting off, with the words to say and what to answer when they ask if it is urgent.

You've been meaning to get it looked at since spring. The knee, the mole, the tooth, the thing that is probably nothing. You know you should ring. You have known for weeks. Every day you don't, it gets a little heavier.

This is not laziness. Booking a doctor's appointment is a chain of small tasks that each need a bit of working memory and a bit of nerve. Find the number. Pick a time you're free. Explain the thing to a stranger. Answer questions you weren't ready for. For an ADHD brain, or a brain running on low battery, that chain is a lot. So the call waits.

Why this is harder than it looks

The appointment is not the problem. You'd happily turn up. The problem is the phone call, and in particular the moment where someone says "and what's it regarding?" and you have to summarise a vague worry on the spot. If you stumble, you feel silly. If you can't remember when it started, you feel sillier. So your brain, quite reasonably, decides not to put you through it.

There's also the practical bit. Surgeries have phone hours. You remember at 9pm. By the time the line opens, the thought has gone.

The fix is to do all the thinking before you dial, so the call itself is just reading.

The five-minute version

Set a timer for five minutes. You're not booking the appointment yet. You're getting ready to.

  1. Write down what it is, in one line. "A mole on my back that's changed shape." "A knee that clicks and aches after stairs." Plain words. The receptionist is not grading you.
  2. Write down since when. A rough month is fine. "Since about March" is a perfectly good answer.
  3. Write down when you can't. Work hours, school pick-up, the days that never work. This stops the "um, let me think" moment that makes you want to hang up.
  4. Write down when you can. Two or three windows. Mornings this week, any afternoon next week. Give them options and they'll find one.
  5. List the places to ring. Your usual surgery first, then a second option if they're full. Leave a space next to each one for "tried". Voicemail counts as tried. An engaged tone counts as tried.
  6. Circle one. That's the one you ring. Not all of them. One.

When the timer goes, you have everything you need on one piece of paper. Now ring the circled one.

The words to say

Have these in front of you so you're reading, not improvising.

Opening the call:

"Hi, I'd like to book an appointment please. It's for a mole on my back that's changed shape. It's been like that since about March. I can do mornings this week or any afternoon next week."

When they ask if it's urgent:

"I don't think it's an emergency, but I'd like it looked at soon. Whatever your next routine slot is would be great."

When there are no slots:

"Could you put me on the cancellation list? And when do you release new appointments, so I know when to ring back?"

That last question matters. It turns "no" into a date in your diary, which is a job your future self can do.

What counts as done

Done is not "appointment booked". Done is "I rang the circled one". If they answer and you book, write the date down while you're still on the line and put it in your phone before you hang up, because that is the moment you will otherwise forget. If it's engaged, try once more. If it's still engaged, or it goes to voicemail, mark it "tried" and stop. You did the hard part today. The paper is still there tomorrow, and tomorrow's call is easier because the words are already written. Call the circled one. If it's engaged, try once more. Then you're done for today.
