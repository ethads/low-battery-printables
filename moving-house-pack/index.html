Moving is a hundred small admin tasks wearing a trench coat and pretending to be one big one. Book a van. Tell the bank. Find boxes. Tell the other bank. Cancel the internet. Work out which cupboard the passports are in. Each job is tiny. Together they're a month of low-grade dread that makes you want to just not move.

If you've been staring at the move date and doing nothing, that's not laziness. It's what happens when a task has no clear first step and about ninety possible ones. The way out is to pick the order in advance and do one short session at a time.

Why this is harder than it looks

Moving has three separate difficulties stacked on top of each other. There's the physical job of packing. There's the admin of telling everyone where you now live. And there's the timing, where some things have to happen weeks out and some can't happen until the day. Trying to hold all three in your head at once is what produces the paralysis.

Split them apart, give each a date, and most of the fear drains out.

The week, one job a day

Seven sessions spread across the month, none over twenty minutes of thinking (the packing itself takes longer, but the deciding doesn't). Miss a session and you do it the next day.

  1. Four weeks out, book the van and the dates. Fix the moving day, book the van or removalists, and book the day off work. Everything else hangs off this.
  2. Three weeks out, tell everyone the new address. Work from a complete list: bank, employer, doctor, dentist, electoral roll, car registration, insurance, subscriptions, the vet. Set up mail redirection so anything you miss still finds you.
  3. Two weeks out, pack the easy rooms. Books, out-of-season clothes, the spare room. Label every box with the room it's going to, not what's inside.
  4. Two weeks out, pack the hard rooms, and the first-night box. Kitchen and bathroom. Then one clearly marked box with sheets, towels, kettle, chargers, toilet paper, medication and a cup. It travels with you, not in the van.
  5. The week before, utilities and the last calls. Electricity, gas, water, internet. Close or transfer each one and take a final meter reading.
  6. Moving day, the one bag and the four photos. One bag with keys, documents, wallet and phone that never leaves your side. Four photos of the empty old place, meters included.
  7. The week after, the sweep. Open the mail redirection pile, update anyone who still has the old address, and change the address on anything that's arrived with it.

The words to say

For a utilities call, have this ready before you dial:

"Hi, I'm moving out on the 14th. I'd like to close the account at my current address and take a final reading that day. Can you confirm what you need from me?"

For a removal quote: "I'm moving a two-bedroom flat on the 14th, ground floor to first floor, about fifteen boxes plus a bed, a sofa and a wardrobe. What would that cost, and is the date available?"

Short, specific, and you can read it off the page.

What counts as done

Done is not an unpacked, finished home. Done is: the date is booked, the address list has been worked through, the boxes are labelled by room, the first-night box exists, the utilities are sorted, you have the four photos, and the week-after sweep has happened. Unpacking can take a month. That's allowed.
