You've hit the wall. Not tired, past that. The to-do list makes you feel sick, replying to a message takes an hour of working up to it, and the one thing everyone suggests, make a plan, is the exact thing that's stopped working. That isn't weakness and it isn't laziness. Planning is a resource, and you've run out of it.

So this isn't a recovery programme and it isn't a productivity system. It's the minimum for a week: the smallest set of actions that keeps the floor under you until you can think again. It isn't therapy or medical advice either. If you're worried about your safety or your health, a doctor or a crisis line comes first, and this can wait.

Why this is harder than it looks

Burnout takes away the exact faculty you'd use to fix it. Deciding what matters, breaking it down, putting it in order: those are planning tasks, and planning is the thing that's broken. So well-meaning advice to just prioritise lands as one more thing you can't do.

The way through is to have the decisions already made. One small job a day, in a fixed order, so you never have to work out what today is. Twenty minutes maximum, and most days far less.

The week, one job a day

Miss a day and do it the next one. The week can be nine days long. Nothing here is a test.

  1. Buy some space. Send the three messages that get you out of things this week: work, a social commitment, a family obligation. Scripts below. You're not explaining, you're informing.
  2. The three things. List the only things that genuinely have to happen this week. Not should. Have to, as in something bad occurs if they don't. It's usually three or fewer. Everything else is officially off.
  3. Floor-level food. Eat, in whatever form takes the least effort. Toast, cereal, a delivery, the same thing three times. Nutrition is next month's problem.
  4. Sleep, at floor level. Not a sleep routine. Just a bedtime you'll actually hit, the phone out of reach, and permission to lie there without it being a failure.
  5. One person. Tell one person you're not okay. Not everyone, not a post. One person, one message.
  6. The admin split. Two columns: can't wait, can wait. Can't wait is rent, medication, anything with a fine attached this week. Everything else goes in the second column and stays there.
  7. The gentle re-entry. Pick one small normal thing for next week. One meeting, one errand, one message to reply to. That's the whole re-entry plan.

The words to say

The space-buying message, for work:

> "I'm not well this week and need to step back from [thing]. I'll be back in touch on [date]. If anything is genuinely urgent, [name] can reach me."

For the one person on day five:

> "I'm struggling a bit at the moment. I don't need anything fixed, I just wanted one person to know. Could you check in with me on [day]?"

Both are complete. You don't owe anyone the backstory.

What counts as done

Done is three messages sent, three things named, food and sleep happening at some level, one person told, and the admin sorted into two piles. If you've eaten and slept and told someone, the week has worked even if the list is half done. The point was never to catch up. The point was to stop falling.
