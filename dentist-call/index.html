How to book a dentist appointment when you haven't been in years and dread the call

It has been long enough now that the number itself is embarrassing. Two years, maybe four. Every time you think about ringing, a small voice asks what the receptionist will say about the gap, and the call goes back on the list.

Here is the thing nobody mentions. Dental receptionists hear "it's been a while" all day long. It is the most ordinary opening line they get. You are not the worst case they have seen this week, or even this morning.

What you're actually avoiding

Usually it is not the teeth. It is three smaller things stacked on top of each other: the phone call, the feeling of being judged, and the fear of what they might find. For a brain running on low battery, three stacked fears is a wall.

So we take the wall apart. The call needs words. The judgement needs an answer ready. The fear of what they'll find can wait until the appointment, which is a different day.

Before you ring: three minutes of notes

Grab a pen and write four short lines.

  1. The reason. "Check-up." Or "a tooth on the left that's sensitive." Or "a filling came out." One line.
  2. Last visit. A rough guess. "Maybe three years." Don't apologise in the note and you won't apologise on the call.
  3. Pain. None, on and off, or constant. This is the one that decides how soon you get seen, so circle it honestly.
  4. When you can come. Early, lunchtime, after work. Give yourself two or three options so you aren't improvising.

Then list two places to try: your old dentist first (they may still have your file), then one backup. Ask each whether they're taking new patients. A "no" there is just information.

The words to say

Have these in front of you.

Booking a routine check-up:

"Hi, I'd like to book a check-up. I haven't been for a while, so I'm not sure what's on file. Do you have anything this week or next?"

If you're in pain:

"I've had a toothache since Tuesday. Is there an emergency or same-day slot?"

If they ask about the gap:

"It's been a few years. I'd just like to get back on track."

That is a complete answer. You don't owe them a reason or an apology.

If you're nervous about the chair:

"I get anxious at the dentist. Could you note that on my file so they go slowly?"

Most practices are glad to be told. It changes how the whole appointment goes, and some will offer a short chat before anything happens.

If it's an emergency

Swelling in your face or jaw, a knocked-out tooth, or pain you can't manage should not wait for a routine slot. Say the word "emergency" at the start, or contact an urgent dental service in your area. This sheet is for the everyday booking, not for those.

What counts as done

Done is not "I have a clean mouth". Done is "I rang the first place on the list". If they answer and you book, write the date and time down while you're still on the line and put it in your phone before you hang up. Ask for a reminder text, too, so the day before takes care of itself.

If it's engaged, try once more. If it goes to voicemail, leave your name, number and the one-line reason, then mark it as tried. That is a real call made. The paper is still there tomorrow and the words are already written.

Don't try to book, fix and floss in one afternoon. The appointment is a different day. Today you make one call.
