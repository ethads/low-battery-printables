How to back up your phone before you lose the photos you can't replace

A fifteen-minute way to check your phone is really backing up, save your photos and contacts in two places, and know what to do if the storage is full.

If you dropped your phone in the sea tonight, what would you lose? For a lot of people the honest answer is years of photos, a handful of messages, and the codes for a few accounts. The phone itself is easy to replace. The rest is not.

You have probably told yourself you will sort the backup out. You may even have turned it on once, long ago. The trouble is that backups fail quietly. A full cloud account, a switched-off setting or a new phone can mean nothing has been saved for months, and you do not find out until it matters.

Why this keeps slipping

A backup is the classic invisible job. Nothing is wrong today. There is no deadline. The reward is something bad not happening, which is hard for any brain to feel. So it loses every time to something louder.

The way to beat that is to do it once, check that it worked, and set a reminder to look again. Fifteen minutes is enough.

Check what you have (5 minutes)

Plug your phone in and connect to wifi. Then look at four things.

  1. What phone is it? Make and model, iPhone or Android.
  2. Is the cloud backup on? On iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then iCloud. On Android, open Settings, then Google, then Backup. Is it switched on, and when did it last run?
  3. Are photos syncing? Open your photo app and look for a sync or backup setting.
  4. Is there space? If your cloud storage is full, backups stop without telling you. Look at the storage bar.

Fix the gaps (10 minutes)

If the backup is off, switch it on and tap "back up now". Leave the phone plugged in and on wifi until the progress bar finishes.

If the cloud is full, you have choices. Pay for the smallest storage plan for a while, or move your photos to a computer or an external drive. Do not delete anything yet. Move first, tidy later.

Make a small table with what needs saving down the side: photos and videos, contacts, messages and chats, notes, authenticator app codes, documents. For each, tick where it is saved: cloud, computer or drive. Anything with no tick is a gap.

To check it worked, open the cloud's website on a computer and look for today's photos. If you can see them there, it works.

One thing people miss

Authenticator apps, the ones that give you login codes, are not always included in a normal backup. If you replace your phone without moving them, you can be locked out of accounts. Before you ever swap or reset the phone, look in the authenticator's own settings for an export or recovery option, and save any backup codes the accounts gave you.

Two copies beat one

Cloud plus a copy on a computer or a drive is safer than either alone. If you can only do one today, do the cloud, then add the second copy when you have more energy. A cloud service can have a problem, and a drive can fail, but both failing on the same day is very unlikely.

What counts as done

Done is a backup that finished today, with the date written down, and photos visible on another device. Then set a reminder every three months to open the backup and check the date. A backup nobody looks at stops working quietly, and the check takes one minute.
