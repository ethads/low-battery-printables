How to book a car service when you don't know anything about cars

How to book a car service or repair without bluffing: what to gather first, how to compare garages, and the plain words to say about price and extra work.

The light has been on for three weeks. Or there is a noise when you brake. Or the service reminder came in the post and went into the pile. You know that you should ring the garage, and you know that you will not know what to say.

Many people put off car things for this reason. It is not the cost that stops you, or not only. It is the fear of sounding clueless, being upsold something you do not understand and agreeing to a bill with no idea what it covers.

The good news is that you do not need to know about cars. You need to know what to say, and a short list of facts you can read from your own paperwork.

What you actually need to know

Garages ask the same four questions. Have the answers in front of you and the call is easy.

  • The car: make, model, year and number plate. All of it is on your registration paperwork or on the plate itself.
  • The distance: roughly how far it has gone. Look at the dashboard. "About sixty thousand" is a perfectly good answer.
  • The symptom: what you see, hear or smell, not what you think the cause is. "A squeal when I brake." "A light on the dashboard that looks like an engine." "A smell like burning when I stop."
  • Last serviced: roughly when, or "I'm not sure". The service book in the glove box often says.

Write these on one piece of paper before you pick up the phone.

Pick two or three garages

Do not ring just one, and do not spend an hour on it. Ask a friend, a neighbour or a local community group which garage they trust. Look at reviews, but read the three-star ones as well as the five-star. Choose two or three that seem sensible.

Make a small table with each garage's name, a price range, the earliest date and whether they offer a courtesy car or a lift. You are comparing, not committing. Pick the one you feel comfortable with, which is not always the cheapest.

The words to say

Have these in front of you. Reading a script is a lot easier than inventing one.

To book: "Hi, I'd like to book my [make and model] in for a [service, check or repair]. It's done about [miles]. Do you have a slot in the next [week or two]?"

If you have a symptom: "I've noticed [the symptom], mainly when [braking, starting or turning]. Could you take a look at that too?"

Before they do any extra work, and this is the most important line: "Please call me with a price before you do anything beyond what we've agreed. I'd like to approve it first."

That last sentence protects you. It keeps the bill from growing without your say. Garages hear it all the time and it does not offend anyone.

When they find something

Sometimes a garage will ring with a list. Take a breath. You do not have to agree on the spot. Useful replies:

"Can you tell me which of those is a safety issue and which could wait?"

"Could you send me that list and the price in writing, so I can think about it?"

"Please go ahead with the first one only, and I'll come back to you about the rest."

Asking for the list in writing and a clear separation between urgent and optional is standard, and it is reasonable.

At pickup

Ask for an itemised invoice and, if you want, to see any parts that were replaced. Keep the invoice with your car papers. If something was fixed, the same noise coming back a week later is a reason to ring them and ask them to look again.

What counts as done

Done is not "the car is fixed". Done is two small things: you rang a garage, and you wrote the booking down. When they give a date and time, put it in your phone while you are still on the line, with a reminder the evening before. Check how you are getting home. If the first garage does not pick up, ring the second. If you only get voicemail, leave a short message with your name, number and the words above. That counts as a try.

A note on safety

If a warning light or noise suggests something dangerous, such as a brake problem, a red warning light, or steam from the engine, do not wait for a convenient appointment. Stop driving when it is safe to do so and contact a garage or breakdown service straight away.
