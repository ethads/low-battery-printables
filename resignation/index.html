How to resign from a job calmly, in writing, without burning bridges

You have decided to leave. Or you are very nearly sure. Either way, the next steps involve a conversation and a letter, and you have probably rehearsed both in your head about forty times.

Leaving a job is allowed. You do not need a perfect reason or a perfect next job to be valid. What you do need is a clear plan for the next few days, because that is where the stress sits. Here it is, in order.

First, look at your contract

Before you say or send anything, read what your contract or staff handbook says about notice. Notice periods differ between jobs and countries, and so do rules about unused leave, final pay and returning equipment. If you cannot find it, ask HR or payroll a plain question: "What is my notice period, and what happens with my final pay and leave?"

Do this first because it sets your last day. Everything else follows from it.

Four things to write down

Take ten minutes with a piece of paper.

  1. Notice. The period your contract gives.
  2. Last day. Today's date plus the notice. Check any holidays or leave you want to use.
  3. What is owed to you. Final pay, unused leave, expenses. Ask payroll or HR to confirm in writing.
  4. What comes next. A new job, a break, or "I do not know". Only you see this box, so write what is true.

Tell your manager first

Where you can, tell your manager in person or on a call before you send anything in writing. It is a courtesy, and it usually goes better. A few plain sentences are enough:

"I wanted you to hear this from me first. I have decided to move on. My last day will be [date]. I will do what I can to make the handover easy."

If they ask why, you can keep it simple:

"It is the right time for me to move on."

You do not have to explain, defend or list grievances. If the conversation heats up, you can say, "I would rather not go into that, but thank you for understanding."

Then put it in writing

A resignation letter is a record, not a place to vent. Keep it short, kind and factual:

"Dear [name], please accept this as formal notice of my resignation from [role]. My last working day will be [date], in line with my notice period. Thank you for the opportunity. I am happy to help with a handover."

Send it by email, and keep a copy. If your employer has a particular process or form, follow that too.

The tidy-up list

Over your last weeks, keep a short list: files to hand over, passwords to pass on, contacts to introduce, personal items to collect. Save your own personal files and contacts to your own devices where that is allowed, but never take anything that belongs to the employer or its clients.

Ask for a reference or a short recommendation while people still remember your best work.

What counts as done

Done is not "I have left". Done is "I told my manager and sent the written notice". After those two things, the rest is paperwork and gradual wrapping up. You can do it in small pieces, ten minutes at a time.

If leaving is because work has become too much, and you are struggling more broadly, a doctor or local support service can help too.
