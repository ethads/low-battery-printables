How to clean out the fridge when you're scared to open it

A ten-minute, shelf-by-shelf way to clear out a fridge you've been avoiding, with one question to decide what goes and no scrubbing required.

There's a moment before you open the fridge where you brace. You know something in there has probably gone off. You don't know what, and you don't know how bad. So you grab the milk, close the door quickly, and add the job to the list of things you'll do on a better day.

That bracing is real. It's part dread, part disgust, and part not knowing how long it will take. Together they make a ten-minute job feel enormous. The fix isn't more motivation. It's a smaller job.

What you're actually doing

You are not cleaning the fridge. You are removing what's clearly finished, so that the rest becomes visible. Wiping the shelves is a bonus, and you can skip it. A fridge that's half emptied is already easier to live with.

Wasted food isn't a moral failure either. It's information about what you actually eat. If three jars of something are growing a coat, you now know you don't need to buy that again.

Before you open the door

Take two minutes to set up, because the worst part of this job is having to stop halfway to find a bag.

  • A bin bag, open, by your feet.
  • A bowl, tray or cool bag for things you're keeping, so they don't warm up while you work.
  • A bowl of hot soapy water and a cloth, for the end.
  • Gloves if you have them. If the smell is the problem, a tea towel over your nose helps.

Open the window, put on a podcast or some music, and set a timer for ten minutes.

One shelf at a time

Start at the top and work down. Take everything off one shelf, decide, put the keepers back, then move on. If you run out of energy after one shelf, stop. That's a result.

For every single item, ask one question: would I eat this off a fork right now? If the answer is no, it goes in the bag. You don't need to read dates, and you don't need to debate it.

It helps to know the items that deserve extra caution, because "it looks okay" isn't a reliable test for them:

  • Cooked rice and leftovers that have been in there for several days.
  • Anything open for more than a week, especially cooked meat or dairy.
  • Anything that smells off, has changed colour, or is bulging or leaking.

The date on the label is guidance, not a verdict. It's a reasonable first check, and your eyes and nose are the second. When you honestly can't tell, bin it. A fresh tub of yoghurt costs far less than a bad night. Jars of pickles, sauces and condiments last a long time, so check the label and use your judgement.

Then handle the salad drawer, the door, and the freezer if you have the energy. The door is usually the easy win, because most of what's in it is sauces and old jars.

The finish

When the bag is full, tie it and take it to the outside bin straight away. Don't leave it by the door. That's the single biggest thing that helps, because the smell is what makes people change their minds.

Then wipe the top shelf with the hot soapy water and put the keepers back where you can see them. Stop there. You can do the rest another day.

What counts as done

Done is a bag in the outside bin and a fridge you can see into. It does not mean a fridge that gleams. Write down one thing to eat first this week, from the keepers, so the clear-out turns into a meal. If you only did one shelf, that counts, and you can do the next shelf tomorrow while the kettle boils.
