How to return the parcel in the hallway before the window closes

A fifteen-minute way to sort the parcels you need to send back, with scripts for a late return and a faulty item, and the trick that gets them posted.

The parcel has been in the hallway for a fortnight. The jeans that didn't fit. The second phone charger you already had. The thing you bought at 1am that seemed essential at the time. You walk past it every day, and every day it costs you a small amount of something. Meanwhile the return window is quietly closing.

This isn't laziness. A return is six separate small jobs wearing one coat: find the order, request the return, print a label, find tape, repack it, get to the post office. Any one of them is nothing. All six, for a thing you already feel a bit silly about buying, is a wall. So the parcel stays.

Why this is harder than it looks

The hard part isn't the post office. It's the sorting. You've probably got more than one parcel, and they're all in different states. One is still inside its window. One shut last Tuesday. One arrived broken and you're not sure what the rules are. One you actually want to keep but haven't unpacked. Your brain sees "the parcels" as a single blob and the blob is too big to pick up.

There's also the printer. Most returns need a label and most printers have an opinion about that. Then the label sits on the desk and the parcel sits in the hallway and they never meet.

Break the blob into piles and the whole thing gets much smaller.

The fifteen-minute version

Set a timer. Gather every unsent parcel into one spot. Then sort.

  1. Four piles. In window, window shut, faulty, keep. Every parcel goes in exactly one. If you're not sure of the window, check the order email and guess the stricter way.
  2. The "keep" pile goes away now. Unpack it, bin the box, done. You've already shrunk the problem.
  3. Write down the "in window" ones, sorted by deadline. Soonest first. Next to each, leave a tick box for "label printed".
  4. Do the "faulty" ones next. Something that arrived broken or not as described is not a normal return. You have consumer rights, and the seller usually has to cover the postage. Email them before you pay for anything.
  5. "Window shut" is a message, not a loss. Many sellers will make an exception if you ask politely, especially if the item is unopened. Send the email and move on. If they say no, the item goes in the keep pile or the charity bag, and you stop paying attention to it.
  6. Print one label. Not all of them. The one with the soonest deadline. Stick it on the parcel immediately.
  7. Put that parcel by the front door. Not the hallway. The door. Parcels by the door get posted on the way out. Parcels in the hallway don't.

When the timer goes, you have one parcel ready to leave the house and a list for the rest.

The words to say

Short, polite, no essay. You're asking, not pleading.

Asking for an exception when the window has shut:

"Hi, I'd like to return order 12345. I know I'm just past the return window. The item is unused and still in its packaging. Would you be able to make an exception and send me a returns label? Thanks very much."

When something arrived faulty:

"Hi, the item from order 12345 arrived faulty. [One sentence about what's wrong.] I'd like a full refund, and as the item isn't as described I'd expect the return postage to be covered. Could you send a prepaid label? Thanks."

That's it. If they say no to the exception, you've lost nothing by asking.

What counts as done

Done is one parcel, label on, by the door. Not every parcel posted. Not a tidy hallway. If you printed one label and stuck it on and moved the parcel to the door, the return is as good as sent, because that parcel will leave the house the next time you do. Everything else is written down by deadline, and the next label is a two-minute job on another day. Print ONE label. Stick it on. Parcel by the door. Done.
