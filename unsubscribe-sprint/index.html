How to unsubscribe from emails without spending your whole evening on it

Open your inbox and look at the number next to it. Four thousand unread, maybe nine thousand. Most of it is not from people. It is shops, newsletters, apps and a gym you left in 2022, all quietly adding to the pile every day.

Clearing that pile is a huge job, and that is why it never gets done. The good news is that you do not have to clear it. You only have to stop it growing. That is a much smaller job, and it takes about fifteen minutes.

Why the usual advice fails

"Just unsubscribe from things as they arrive" sounds sensible. In practice it means making a small decision every time an email lands, and a brain on low battery has no spare decisions. So you skip it, and the next one arrives.

The other usual advice is "get to inbox zero", which treats the pile as the problem. The pile is only the symptom. The cause is the senders, and there are far fewer senders than emails. Forty senders can produce four thousand messages.

The sprint, step by step

Set a fifteen minute timer. You are going after senders, not emails.

  1. Search for the word "unsubscribe". Type it into your inbox search. Every result is a sender who has put that word in their footer, which means every result is a mailing list.
  2. Sort or group by sender. In most mail apps you can sort by sender name. The names that appear again and again are your loudest ones. Write the first ten on a piece of paper.
  3. Open one email from the first name. Scroll to the very bottom. Press the unsubscribe link. If it asks for a reason, pick anything. If it asks you to confirm, confirm.
  4. Cross the name off the paper. The crossing out matters. It is the only reward this job offers, so take it.
  5. Repeat down the list. Stop at ten, even if you feel brilliant. Ten fewer senders is a real difference. Ten more can wait for next week.
  6. Do not read any of the emails. This is where sprints go wrong. You open a newsletter to unsubscribe and end up reading it. Unsubscribe first. If it matters, it will find you another way.

When the link does not work

Some senders hide the link, or it leads to a page that errors. You have two options.

First, use your mail app's own button. Many apps show an unsubscribe option at the very top of the email, and it works even when the link in the footer does not.

Second, use the words below. Reply to the sender, or use their contact form:

"Please remove this email address from your mailing list. I have tried the unsubscribe link and I am still receiving emails. Thank you."

If they are still writing after a week, mark the message as junk or spam. That teaches your inbox to hide them, and it costs you nothing.

A note on the "important" ones

You will hit a few senders you are not sure about. A bank, a school, a utility. Leave those alone for today. The sprint is for shops, newsletters and anything you have never read. If you are unsure, skip it and move to the next name. Skipping is allowed.

If you want to keep a few newsletters you actually enjoy, keep them. The aim is a quieter inbox, not a silent one.

What counts as done

Done is not an empty inbox. Done is ten names crossed off a piece of paper. Write the number at the bottom, and then work out roughly how many emails a week those senders were sending you. That is the number of emails you will never see again, and it is usually a lot bigger than you expect.

Then close the inbox and walk away. The old emails are still there tomorrow. The difference is that there will be fewer new ones.

Pick a day next week and do ten more if you feel like it. If you don't, nothing breaks. You have already made a measurable difference to how your inbox feels.
