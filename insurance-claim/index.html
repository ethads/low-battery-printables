Something broke, got stolen, or got damaged, and you have been meaning to deal with the insurance for longer than you would like to admit. Every time you think about it you feel a small drop. There will be forms. There will be questions. There might be an excess. You do not know where the policy is.

So the damaged thing sits in the corner, and the claim sits in your head.

What is actually in the way

Most of the weight is uncertainty. You do not know what they will ask, you do not know if you will say something wrong, and you worry the call will go badly and you will have made things worse. Added to that, making a claim feels a bit like admitting something went wrong, which is an unpleasant feeling to walk into on a low-energy day.

Here is the reassuring part. Claims lines exist to take calls like yours. The person on the other end has handled thousands. They are not looking for a reason to catch you out. They are looking for facts.

Gather the facts first

Do this part away from the phone. Set a timer for ten minutes.

  1. What happened, in one line. "The kitchen window broke in the storm." Not why, not whose fault. Just what.
  2. When and where. The date, a rough time, the place.
  3. What was affected. Make a short list: the item, roughly how old, roughly what it costs to replace. A rough number is fine for now.
  4. Proof. Take photos of the damage before you move anything. Look for receipts, order emails, or a report number if you told the police or another body.
  5. Your policy details. The insurer's name and the policy number. They are on the email you got when you bought it, or on the card in your wallet.

Stick to what you know. If they ask what caused it and you do not know, "I don't know" is a perfect answer. Guessing about cause or blame can cause trouble later.

The call

Read from your page.

"Hi, I'd like to make a claim on my [home, contents, car or travel] policy, number [policy number]. On [date], [what happened] and [item] was damaged."

When they ask about cover or costs:

"Can you tell me what my excess is, what's covered, and what you need from me next?"

Before you hang up:

"Can I have a claim reference number, please? And can you email me the steps?"

Write the reference number and the name of the person you spoke to straight away, before you do anything else. That is the number you will need for every later conversation.

Things that help

  • Do not throw the damaged item away until the insurer says you can. They may want to see it or a photo.
  • If it is not safe to leave things as they are, make the situation safe first and photograph before and after.
  • If something was stolen, tell the police or the relevant body in your country as well. The insurer may need a report number.
  • Ask what the deadlines are. Many policies need you to report within a certain time.

What counts as done

Done is not "claim paid". Done is "claim opened and a reference number written down". That is the hardest step, and it is a single phone call or a form online. Everything after it is the insurer sending you steps, which you can do one at a time. If the call goes to a queue, chat or an online form works just as well, with the same words typed in.

Every policy is different, and this is not advice on whether you should claim or what will be paid. If you are unsure, you can ask the insurer directly or contact a free consumer advice service in your country.
