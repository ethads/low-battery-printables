Did you know about Thursday? You said yes to something in a text weeks ago. There is a letter on the fridge. There is a booking email you opened on the bus and then closed. None of these are in your calendar, so all of them are in your head, and your head, if we are honest, is not a reliable filing cabinet.

Missing a date feels like a personal failing. It is not. It is a systems problem. If a date lives in four places and none of them sends you an alert, you will miss it sometimes, however much you care.

Why dates slip

Time is invisible for a lot of people, especially with ADHD or a tired brain. "Next Tuesday" sounds close until it is yesterday. On top of that, dates arrive from everywhere: messages, emails, paper, a casual comment in the corridor. Writing each one into a calendar is a small job, and small jobs are the ones that slide.

So the fix is not to remember harder. The fix is a one-off collecting trip, and then a habit that takes two minutes a week.

The collecting trip

Set a fifteen minute timer. You are copying dates, not planning your life.

  1. Check your messages. Search for words like "on", "at", "see you" and "booked" in texts and chats. Anything you said yes to goes on the list.
  2. Check your email. Search for "appointment", "booking", "invite" and "reminder" over the last month. You will find at least two you had forgotten.
  3. Check the fridge. Paper notes, school letters, a card from the dentist. Photograph or copy them.
  4. Check your head. Is there anything you are quietly dreading in the next month? Write that down too. Once it is on paper it can stop circling.
  5. Make one master list. Date, what it is, time and place, and anything you need to bring or book. Soonest first.
  6. Put each one in your phone. Right now, one by one. Add an alert. The next seven days get an alert the evening before as well as on the day.

Copy, do not think. If you do not know what it is, write "find out what this is" and move on.

What to do about clashes

When you put everything in one place, you will probably find a double-booking. That is a good thing to find now, instead of on the day. You have three easy choices.

If you need to cancel:

"Hi, I need to cancel my appointment on [date] at [time]. Could I rebook for [date] or the next available time?"

If you need to decline politely:

"Thanks so much for the invite. I can't make it this time, but I hope it goes brilliantly."

If you are double-booked with a friend:

"I've just realised I've got two things on the [date]. Could we move ours to [day] instead?"

People are much kinder about a change made a week ahead than a no-show.

The two minute weekly look

A calendar only works if you look at it. Pick one regular time for a two minute look. Sunday evening is popular, or Monday with your first coffee. Scroll the next two weeks. Check each date has what it needs: a booking, a lift, a gift, a form.

Set it as a recurring alert named CALENDAR so you do not rely on remembering. That is the only habit this system needs.

Things that make it stick

Use one calendar, not three. If you have a work calendar and a personal one, make sure they show in the same app. Name entries clearly. "Dentist, 3pm, bring card" is better than "appointment". Add the address or a link in the notes so you are not hunting for it on the day.

What counts as done

Done is not a perfect calendar. Done is every date you found sitting in your phone with an alert. Write down how many you added and how many clashes you found. Pick the soonest date and check it is right.

Then stop. You have moved all those dates out of your head, and that is a bigger relief than it sounds.
