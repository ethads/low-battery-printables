How to cancel a subscription or membership when you keep putting it off

A short routine for cancelling a gym, phone plan or subscription you don't want, with the message to send and what to say when they offer you a deal.

You decided to cancel it a while ago. The gym you haven't visited, the streaming service, the phone plan, the app that started as a free trial. And then a month went by and you paid for it again.

This is one of the most common admin traps there is, and it isn't because you're bad with money. Cancelling is designed to be a little awkward. The button hides, the form asks for a reason, the call ends with a discount offer. For a brain that avoids small conflicts, each of those is a reason to wait until next week.

Four things to find first

Spend four minutes finding these. They turn "I should cancel that" into a real task.

  1. What it is. The company and the plan. "Gym membership, monthly."
  2. Account details. The name and email or phone number it's under, and any member number. This is what they'll ask for first.
  3. The next charge. Look at your bank or the app. Write the date and the amount. That is your deadline, and often the cheapest day to act.
  4. The contract. Is it rolling, a fixed term, or does it need notice? If you aren't sure, that's a question for the message, not a reason to stop.

Pick the easiest way out

There are usually five routes, and you only need one that works:

  • Account settings page
  • Email or contact form
  • Web chat
  • Phone
  • Post or in person

Try them in that order. Settings pages are quickest, and email or chat leaves a record, which is a good thing. A phone call can be fine, but you can't prove what was said. If the settings page hides the option, search "[company name] cancel" and it usually turns up.

Tick the first one that works and stop there. You don't need to try them all.

The message to send

Copy this and fill in the gaps:

"Hello, I'd like to cancel my [service] account, [name and number]. Please cancel it from today, or the earliest date allowed, and confirm the end date and that no further payments will be taken."

If they offer a discount or ask why:

"Thank you, but I don't want to continue. Please go ahead with the cancellation."

You can say that line twice, three times, as many as it takes. Repeating is allowed. It isn't rude.

If they say you're in a contract:

"Can you tell me the exact end date and any cost to leave early, in writing?"

That turns a vague worry into a number and a date, which you can then decide about. Rules about contracts and notice vary by country and by company, so ask for the details in writing rather than guessing.

Make it stick

The cancellation is only real when you can show it. Take a screenshot of the confirmation, or save the email in a folder called DONE. If no confirmation arrives within a day, send the message again on the same thread.

Then look at your next statement. If a payment appears after the end date, reply to the same conversation with the date of your cancellation and ask for it back. Having the screenshot means you're not relying on anyone's memory.

What counts as done

Done is not "everything I'm paying for is perfect". Done is one cancellation message sent, with the confirmation saved. If you have five things to cancel, do the one with the nearest charge date first and stop. Tomorrow's you can do the next.

If you freeze at the sending, that is fine. Paste the message into the box, close your eyes, and press the button. The paper is not judging you.
