How to sort the admin for your side hustle without it taking over your life

Quick test. If someone asked how much your side hustle made last month, could you tell them in under a minute? And do you know whether you are supposed to tell anyone about it?

If the honest answer is "not really", you are in very good company. People who start a side hustle usually love the work and dread the paperwork. The money arrives in the same account as the rent, the receipts live in six places and the invoice you meant to send in March is still in your head.

Why the admin piles up

Side work has no office, no accounts team and no one to remind you. You are the business and the admin department, and the admin department only works on days when you have spare energy. That is the point where a tired brain says "later" and the pile begins.

The fix is not to become a different person. It is to turn admin into a handful of small, boring jobs that fit into a week.

The week, one job a day

Twenty minutes at the most each day.

  1. Monday: separate the money. Open a second account or a named pot for hustle money and costs. This single step makes every later job easier.
  2. Tuesday: log what comes in. A page with four columns: date, who paid, what for, amount. Fill in the last month or two.
  3. Wednesday: log what goes out. Another page for costs. Save receipt photos in an album and forward email receipts to a folder.
  4. Thursday: find out what you must do. Look up your country's tax office or revenue service and read what it says about side income. Write down thresholds, deadlines and questions.
  5. Friday: set your prices and terms. A one-page price list, how and when you want to be paid and what happens if work is cancelled.
  6. Saturday: send one invoice. Or chase one that is overdue. The words are below.
  7. Sunday: set a weekly slot. Ten minutes, same time every week, written down.

The words to say

To ask the bank about a separate account:

"Hi, I do some work on the side and I'd like a separate account or pot to keep that money apart from my personal money. What options do you have, and are there any fees?"

To send an invoice:

"Hi [name], attached is the invoice for [job]. Payment is due by [date]. Let me know if you need anything else. Thanks again."

To chase one:

"Hi [name], a quick reminder that invoice [number] for [amount] was due on [date]. Could you let me know when I can expect payment? Thank you."

To ask a professional about the rules:

"Hi, I earn some money on the side from [type of work]. I'd like to know what I need to register or report, and by when. Could you point me to the right information or book me a short call?"

Ask the right people about the rules

What you must register, report and pay tax on depends on your country, your income and how you earn it. Nobody can tell you that in a blog post. The tax office, a qualified accountant or a free small-business advice service can, and many offer a short first conversation at no cost. Guessing is the expensive way to learn.

You do not need to be scared of the question. People ask it every day.

Keep it simple

A messy log you keep up beats a beautiful system you abandon. Use a notebook, a notes app or a basic spreadsheet. Name everything HUSTLE so you can find it. Keep receipts in one album, invoices in one folder and one page on top saying where everything lives.

What counts as done

On Monday, done is a separate pot. On Saturday, done is an invoice sent. By Sunday, done is a weekly ten-minute slot in your calendar and a page that says where your money, logs and prices live. You do not need to be an accountant. You need to be findable by yourself.

If a day slips, do it the next day. The week can be nine days long.
