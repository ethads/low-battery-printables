What baby admin to do first when you are a sleep-deprived new parent

The baby is here. Everyone says it is the best thing that has ever happened. It also arrives with a stack of forms, a list of appointments, an employer who needs a date, a stream of messages from people who want to visit and a version of you who has not had more than two hours of sleep in a row.

Nobody mentions the admin until you are standing in the kitchen at three in the morning, holding a baby in one hand and a letter in the other, trying to work out whether it matters.

First, a few honest things

You do not have to do it all this week. Most of it has more time than you fear. The baby comes first, and your recovery comes second. Ask your partner, a friend or a relative to take a day of this. A fair share of the admin is not a favour.

Rules about registration, leave, benefits and insurance differ by country, so this article will not tell you what you must do where you live. It will show you how to find out quickly and get it done in small pieces. If anything is urgent for your health or the baby's, call your midwife, doctor or emergency line first. Admin can wait. They cannot.

The week in seven short jobs

Each job is twenty minutes at most, ideally during a settled moment. Miss a day and do it the next one.

  1. Find out what is due. Look at the hospital or midwife paperwork and the official government site for your country. Write a list of everything that might be needed, and beside each, "deadline" or "no deadline". This one page will probably calm you down more than anything else.
  2. Register the birth. Usually the first official step and the one that unlocks others. Check who can register, what documents you need, whether you can book online and how to get copies of the certificate.
  3. Health appointments. Book the next check for the baby and one for you. Yes, you need one. If you have no family doctor or paediatrician yet, ask for recommendations.
  4. Insurance and money support. Many plans need the baby added within a set time. Check the policy, and look on the official site for any parent or child support you may qualify for. Start the one most likely to matter.
  5. Tell work. Find your leave policy and send a short, factual email. Words below.
  6. Visitors, messages and thank-yous. Set the terms once, in one message.
  7. One folder. Paperwork, photos of it and one page of key dates, in one place.

Words to use

To the hospital or midwife: "Could you tell me which forms and registrations we need to complete after the birth, and which ones have a deadline? Is there a leaflet or website we should use?"

To the insurer: "We've had a baby and want to add them to our plan. Could you tell me how long we have, what documents you need and whether the cover starts from the birth date?"

To your employer: "Hi [name], I wanted to let you know that our baby arrived on [date]. I'd like to confirm the arrangements for my leave. Could you tell me what paperwork you need and who I should send it to? I'm planning to be away from [date] until about [date], and I'll confirm as things become clearer."

To visitors: "We're so happy to share our news. We're resting and getting to know the baby, so we'll let you know when we're ready for visitors. If you'd like to help, a meal or a quick errand would be wonderful."

Saying no is allowed

You do not owe anyone your energy this week. "Not this week, but soon" is a full sentence. Ask one person to be the contact for updates, so you are not replying to fifty messages. Thank-you notes can be three lines and weeks late.

What counts as done

Each day has one thing that counts: the list written, the registration booked, the check in the diary, the insurer contacted, the email sent, the message sent, the folder labelled. Done is not perfect. Done is that the next step is written down and someone knows where it is.

Put every paper into one folder labelled BABY, photograph each one, and keep one page of key dates at the front. Tell your partner or a trusted person where it all lives.

Looking after yourself

If you notice that you feel very low, anxious or not like yourself for more than a couple of weeks after the birth, talk to your doctor or midwife. That is part of the admin of looking after the family, and you do not have to manage it alone.
