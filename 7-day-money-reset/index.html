You haven't opened the banking app in a while. You have a rough idea of the number, and the rough idea is scary enough that the real one can wait. Meanwhile the subscriptions keep going out, the bill you're dreading sits unopened, and the not-knowing hums quietly in the background of every purchase.

That isn't irresponsibility. Money avoidance is what happens when every look at the account has historically ended in a bad feeling. The brain learns that the app hurts, and steers you away from it. Budgets and spreadsheets don't help, because they ask you to look at everything at once, which is exactly the thing you can't do.

Why this is harder than it looks

Money admin is five or six separate jobs wearing one coat. Knowing the balance is one job. Knowing what leaves each month is another. Finding the subscriptions, moving a payment date, ringing about the bill, setting something aside. Each one needs a different kind of nerve, and the usual advice is to do them all on a Sunday afternoon with a spreadsheet open. That's a lot of nerve for one afternoon.

Spread over a week, each day only asks for a single look. Twenty minutes, one job, then the app goes away until tomorrow.

The week, one job a day

Nothing on this list takes longer than twenty minutes. Miss a day and do it the next day. A nine-day week is fine.

  1. Monday: look at the number. Open the app, read the balance, write it on paper, close the app. You don't fix anything today. You just know.
  2. Tuesday: list what's leaving. Go down last month's statement and write every regular payment on one page with its amount and date.
  3. Wednesday: find the leaks. Circle anything on that list you'd forgotten, don't use, or pay twice for. Cancel one.
  4. Thursday: move the dates. Pick the payment that lands at the worst time and ask to shift it to just after payday.
  5. Friday: ring about the bill. The scary one. Words below. Fifteen minutes and it has a plan instead of a shadow.
  6. Saturday: one pound a day. Set up one small automatic transfer to a separate pot. Tiny on purpose. The habit is the point.
  7. Sunday: write the one page. The number, what leaves, what you cancelled, what you moved, what the bill plan is. One page you can look at next month instead of the app.

The words to say

Ringing about money is the day most people skip, so the words are ready. For a bill you can't cover:

"Hi, I've got a bill due on [date] and I won't be able to pay all of it this month. What options are there for a payment plan?"

For moving a payment date:

"Hi, could I move my monthly payment date to the [day]? It would land just after I'm paid, which makes it easier to keep up."

For cancelling a subscription that's making it hard:

"I'd like to cancel please, effective from the next billing date. Could you confirm that by email?"

None of these need an explanation. People ring with these requests every day, and the person answering has a button for each one.

What counts as done

Done on Monday is a number written down. Done on Friday is a bill with a plan, even if the plan is "ring back next month". By Sunday, done is one page in your own handwriting that tells you where you stand, what leaves, what you've plugged and what's coming. Not a budget. Not a spreadsheet. Just the knowing, which is the thing you didn't have a week ago.
