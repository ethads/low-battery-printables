How to get back on track with your studies when you are behind and avoiding the portal

A week of study admin for students who have fallen behind: deadlines, an extension request, a tutor email, enrolment checks and a plan you can follow.

You have not opened the student portal in weeks. It is not that you do not care about the course. It is that opening it means seeing everything at once: the deadline that passed, the email from your tutor, the reading, the form about fees. So it is easier to leave it shut, which makes it worse.

If this is you, you are in good company. Tutors and student services see students in this exact spot every term, and they would much rather hear from you late than not at all. Here is a way back that does not need a big burst of energy.

What is really stuck

It is rarely the learning. It is the admin around the learning: logging in, finding dates, asking for help, checking money. Each is small and a bit scary, and none of them feels like studying, so they never get done.

The answer is to treat the admin as its own project for one week. You are not catching up on the course yet. You are finding out where you stand and asking for what you need.

The week

One job a day, about twenty minutes.

  1. Monday: log in and look. Just look. Write each course and one word beside it: fine, worried or panic.
  2. Tuesday: list every deadline. One page: course, task, due date, started or not. Circle the next three.
  3. Wednesday: ask for the extension. For the most pressing item, using the words below.
  4. Thursday: email a tutor. One question or one request.
  5. Friday: check enrolment and money. Fees, deadlines, forms, funding.
  6. Saturday: build a weekly plan. Three study blocks you can actually keep.
  7. Sunday: set a tiny routine. A ten-minute portal check each week.

Words for the two scary messages

An extension request:

"Hi [name], I am writing to ask for an extension on [assignment], due [date]. I have [what you have done] and could submit by [new date]. Is this possible, and is there a form I should complete?"

If you have already missed it:

"Hi [name], I missed the deadline for [assignment] and I am sorry. I would like to submit by [date] if that is allowed. What do you advise?"

An email to a tutor:

"Hi [name], I have fallen behind in [course] and I would like to get back on track. Could we have a short chat, or could you tell me what to prioritise?"

You do not need to explain private circumstances unless a form asks for them. Many places have a student support or advice service that can talk through options, such as a later deadline, a lighter load or a break, without judgement.

Making a plan you will keep

On Saturday, resist the urge to design the perfect timetable. A plan that assumes you will be a different person next week will fail. Choose three blocks of 30 to 45 minutes, put them in your calendar, and add one spare slot for the day you miss one. Studying alongside someone, in person or on a call, helps many people begin.

What counts as done

Done is not "I have caught up". Done is "I know my deadlines, I have asked for help and I have a plan for next week". That is a huge shift from avoiding the portal. The coursework can now be tackled in small pieces, one at a time.

If studying feels impossible in a wider way, speak to a doctor or your institution's support service about your options.
