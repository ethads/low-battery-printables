The boxes are unpacked, or at least open. The kettle works. And somewhere in the back of your head is a quiet voice listing all the places that still think you live at your old address.

It is a long list. Bank, doctor, employer, insurers, subscriptions, the gym you never go to, the charity that sends you letters. Trying to do them all in one go is how people end up doing none.

Why it feels so big

Each place wants a slightly different thing. One has a form, one has an app, one needs a phone call and a security question. None of them are hard on their own. Together they are a long run of small, boring decisions, and boring decisions are exactly what a tired brain avoids.

There is also no clear finish line. You can never be sure you have told everyone. That makes it hard to stop, and so hard to start.

Change the question

Instead of "tell everyone", ask "tell the ones that would hurt most if they got it wrong, today". Letters from your bank, your doctor and your insurers matter. A catalogue from a clothes shop does not.

Here is the order that works for most people.

  1. Post redirection. If your postal service offers it, set it up first. It gives you weeks to catch everything else, because mail follows you while you work through the list.
  2. Money and ID. Bank, cards, tax or government office, driving licence.
  3. Health. Doctor, dentist, any regular clinic.
  4. Work. Your employer's payroll or HR system.
  5. Insurers. Home, contents, car, phone. A wrong address can affect cover, so tell them in good time.
  6. Bills and subscriptions. Power, internet, phone, streaming, anything that bills monthly.
  7. Everything else. Memberships, shops, charities, friends. Do these as the post arrives.

Get ready in two minutes

Write down your old address, your new address with the postcode, and the date you moved. You will type these a lot. Copy them from one source, so there are no typos from place to place. Keep a note of the email and phone number each place uses to check it is you.

The words to say

For places with no online form:

"Hi, I've moved and I need to update my address on my account. The account is under [name], [account or reference number]. My new address is [new address]. I moved on [date]. Could you confirm by email once it's updated?"

For redirecting post:

"Hi, I'd like to set up mail redirection from [old address] to [new address], starting [date]."

Most places do not need a reason, a letter or a lot of detail. They need the two addresses and a date.

Keep it to one list

Draw a table: who, how, date told, tick. Fill in the first row only. Do not plan the rest. Once the first is ticked, the second row stops looking scary, and you can do a few a day as you notice them.

What counts as done

Done for today is one row ticked, ideally the bank or your doctor. Done for the week is the redirect set up and the first three rows complete. Done forever does not exist, because there will be one or two you forget. They turn up in the post, and when they do, you tell them then. Check how long your postal redirect lasts and set a reminder before it ends, so you are not caught out when it stops.
