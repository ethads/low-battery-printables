How to deal with clothes all over the floor when you can't face laundry

Get the clothes off the floor in fifteen minutes without folding anything, with three bags, a chair, one wash load and the alarm that stops the mould.

The clothes are on the floor. Not a few. A layer. Some are clean, some are dirty, some you wore for an hour and could wear again, and you can't tell which is which without picking each one up and sniffing it, so you don't. You step over the pile to get to bed. People call it a floordrobe, which is a kinder word than it feels like.

This isn't slovenliness. Laundry is a chain of tasks spread over hours, with gaps in between where you have to remember to come back. Sort, load, wait, move, wait, fold, put away. For an ADHD brain, the gaps are where it dies. The wash finishes, you don't hear it, two days later it smells and you have to do it again. After a few rounds of that, the floor starts to seem like the sensible option.

Why this is harder than it looks

Two things make the floordrobe stick. The first is the sorting. Clean and dirty have merged, and your brain treats the whole pile as one undifferentiated job. The second is folding. Somewhere you picked up the rule that clothes have to be folded to count as put away, and folding a whole floor's worth is an hour you don't have, so none of it gets put away.

Drop both rules. Nothing gets folded today. "Worn once, still fine" is a real category and you're allowed to use it. The floor being clear is the whole goal. Not the drawers. Not the wardrobe. The floor.

The fifteen-minute version

Set a timer for fifteen minutes. You need three bags (bin bags are fine) and a chair.

  1. Label the bags in your head: wash, wear again, done with. The chair is "put away".
  2. Pick up each item once and make a snap call. Smells or stained, wash bag. Worn once and fine, wear-again bag. Clean and you're keeping it, chair. Doesn't fit or you never wear it, done-with bag. Don't deliberate. Five seconds per item.
  3. Keep going until the floor is clear or the timer rings. Whichever comes first. If the timer wins, that's fine. The bags stay where they are and you pick up tomorrow.
  4. The chair pile goes into drawers, unfolded. Any drawer. Open it, put the clothes in, close it. You can fold on a day when folding feels possible. Today is not that day and that's allowed.
  5. The wear-again bag hangs on a hook or the back of a door. That's its home now. It's not the floor.
  6. The done-with bag goes by the front door with a sticky note saying where it's going, charity or a friend. It leaves next time you do.
  7. Start one wash load. One. Fill the machine from the wash bag, start it, and set an alarm on your phone for when it finishes. Call the alarm MOVE IT.

That alarm is the most important thing on this list.

The load plan

One load at a time, finished properly, beats three loads that go smelly in the drum. The plan is simple: one load goes in, the alarm goes on, and when it rings you move the washing to the dryer or the airer straight away, before you do anything else. Only then does the next load go in.

The MOVE IT alarm is the habit that breaks the mouldy-washing loop. You don't have to remember. The phone remembers. When it goes off, you move the clothes. That's the whole habit, and it works because it takes the remembering out of your hands.

What counts as done

Done is the floor clear and one wash load started. Not the washing finished. Not a single thing folded. Not the wardrobe sorted. If you can see the carpet and the machine is running with an alarm set, you've done the job. The wash bag with the rest of the dirty clothes can wait by the machine for the next load, and tomorrow's load is easy because the sorting is already done. Start ONE wash load. Set the alarm. Done.
