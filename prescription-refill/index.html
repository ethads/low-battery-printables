How to stop running out of your prescription when remembering the refill is hard

A simple system for ordering repeat prescriptions before the last tablet: a counting method, a reminder rule and words for the surgery and pharmacy.

Tonight you take the last tablet. You knew it was getting low. You noticed it last week, thought "I'll sort that tomorrow", and then tomorrow turned into seven of them.

If this is you, it is probably not carelessness. Ordering a repeat is a small chain of jobs: notice, count, check you're allowed another, contact someone, collect it. Each link is easy to drop when your attention is stretched, and the whole thing only becomes urgent when it's nearly too late.

One thing first. Never stop a medicine because the refill got hard. If you've already run out, ring your pharmacy or doctor's surgery today and say so. They deal with this every day and can often help the same day.

Count in days, not pills

The most useful habit is changing what you count. Pills are a poor unit because they depend on the dose. Days are what you actually care about.

Do this once, now, with every box and bottle in front of you:

  1. Count what's left.
  2. Divide by how many you take a day. That is your days left.
  3. Add that to today's date. That's the run-out date.
  4. Put a reminder in your phone for seven days before the run-out date. Name it ORDER MEDS.

Seven days of margin is the whole trick. It turns a panic into a routine errand.

One page for everything you take

Write a line for each medicine, copied straight from the label:

  • Name and dose
  • Days left
  • Where it comes from (surgery, hospital, a particular pharmacy)
  • Date you ordered it

Then take a photo of each label and keep the pictures in a folder called MEDS. Now you can read a name and dose to anyone, any time, without hunting for the box.

Check two other things while you're at it: how many repeats are left, and when a doctor last reviewed the medicine. A lot of prescriptions need a check-up every so often. If yours is overdue, that is why the refill is stalling, and the fix is to book the review.

The words to use

To the surgery:

"Hi, I'd like to order a repeat of [medicine and dose]. I have about five days left. Can you tell me when it will be ready?"

If they say you need a review first:

"Could I book that now? And is there any way to get a short supply so I don't run out in between?"

To the pharmacy:

"Could you tell me when my [medicine] will be ready, and could you text me when it is?"

It is also worth asking, once, whether they offer automatic repeats or reminder texts. Many do, and it takes the job off your plate entirely.

What counts as done

Done is not a full cupboard. Done is "I ordered the one that runs out first". If you have three medicines due, do the earliest and let the others wait until tomorrow. Write the order date on your page and move the next reminder forward.

If you can't get through on the phone, an online form, an app or a message to the practice works the same way. Same words, typed.

A calmer month

After the first time, the routine shrinks. Every month you do a thirty-second check against your page, tick the order date, set the next reminder, and that's it. Some people pair it with a regular event, like payday or the first Sunday of the month, so it has a hook to hang on.

If a gap does happen, it isn't a failure. It's a sign the margin was too small. Make it ten days next time and carry on.
