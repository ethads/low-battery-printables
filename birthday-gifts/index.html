It is half past eight on Friday night and you have just remembered the party is tomorrow. You are standing in a petrol station holding a card with a dog on it, wondering whether a gift voucher is acceptable. It is. But you would rather not be here.

Forgetting birthdays does not mean you do not care. Most of the people it happens to care a great deal, and that is exactly why it hurts. The trouble is that dates, ideas and shopping time all live in different places, and none of them tells you when to act.

What goes wrong

A good gift needs three things at once: the date, an idea and some time to buy it. You might have one of the three. Usually the idea turns up in March when you cannot buy it and is gone by September when you can.

The other problem is scale. When you think about everyone you might buy for across the whole year, the list feels enormous, so you avoid it altogether. The answer is to plan only the next three months.

The ten minute list

Set a timer for ten minutes and grab a page.

  1. Write down who has something coming in the next four weeks. These are the urgent ones. Birthdays, anniversaries, baby showers, thank-you gifts.
  2. Write down the next month after that. You only need names for now. Ideas can come later.
  3. Write down the month after that. Names only again.
  4. Add the people with no date. Someone you love but cannot place the birthday for. Ask a mutual friend, or scroll an old message to find it.
  5. Give the first group an idea each. One line. "Candle." "Book about gardening." "Coffee voucher." You are allowed obvious ideas. Obvious is good.
  6. Add a budget and an order-by date. If it needs posting, the order-by date is a week before. If it is local, three days before.
  7. Circle the nearest date. That is the one you act on today.

Then open a shop, put the nearest present in the basket and press order. The others can wait for their turn.

If you are already late

A late present is still a present. A late message is still a kind message. The thing that hurts is silence, not lateness. Here are words that work.

For a belated birthday:

"Happy belated birthday! I'm so sorry I'm late. I wanted to say it properly: I'm really glad you're in my life."

For when you have no idea what to buy:

"Hi [name], I'd love to get [person] something they'll like. Any ideas on what they're into at the moment?"

For when you have no time to buy anything:

"I'd love to take you out for a coffee on me. Are you free [day]?"

An experience, a meal you cook or a call that lasts an hour is often better than an object.

A small stock cupboard

One trick saves more last-minute panic than any list. Buy two plain cards and two generic presents now, then keep them in a drawer. A good candle, a nice notebook, a bar of chocolate that is not terrible. When a date sneaks up on you at 8pm, you are not empty-handed.

Check the stock every few months. Replace what you have used. It is cheaper than a panic purchase and kinder to your nerves.

Keep the list somewhere you can see it

A list in a drawer will be forgotten. Put the page on the fridge, or take a photo and set it as a note on your phone. Add each date to your calendar with an alert one week ahead. That is the alert that gives you time to order.

What counts as done

Done is one present ordered, or one message written, not the whole list. Write down which one. Then write the next date on the page and set an alert for it.

If you manage only the nearest one, that is still a good evening.
