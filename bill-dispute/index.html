How to question a bill that looks wrong when you'd rather just pay it and move on

The bill is higher than last time. Or there is a line on it you don't recognise. Or you are fairly sure you were charged twice.

Part of you wants to pay and forget, because it's quicker than the alternative. Another part knows the alternative is a few minutes of work that could save real money. Most people go with the first part, then feel a little sick every month.

Here is a way to make the second part easy.

A small reframe first

Questioning a bill is not an accusation. You aren't saying anyone did anything wrong. You are asking them to check a number. Companies get this request constantly, and they have a team and a form for it. A polite message is the most boring thing they'll read all day.

One thing to avoid: ignoring a bill because it's wrong. Even if you're certain it's an error, tell them, in writing, before the due date if you can. Silence can look like agreement.

Five minutes: what looks off

Open the bill and write down four things:

  1. Who and what. The company, the account number, the bill date and the total.
  2. The problem. Which line is wrong? Charged twice, higher than usual, a service you didn't get, a fee you don't recognise.
  3. What it should be. Your best estimate. If you don't know, write "please explain it". Both are fine.
  4. The due date. Put it in your phone now.

Five more minutes: compare

Get last month's bill, or your own records, and put the two side by side. Make a list with a line for each item: what was charged, what you expected, and the difference. Mistakes tend to jump out once you do this: a new fee, a doubled line, a changed rate, a usage reading that looks too high.

Highlight what's different and note the amounts. That list is your evidence. You don't need a spreadsheet, just a few lines on paper.

The message to send

Copy this, filling in the gaps:

"Hello, I'm writing about account [number], bill dated [date] for [amount]. I believe there is an error: [one sentence]. Could you please review it and send me a corrected bill or an explanation?"

Then add:

"Please confirm in writing that the disputed amount is on hold while you check, and tell me when I can expect an answer."

Whether a hold is possible depends on the company and your country, so ask rather than assume. If there is part of the bill you agree with, pay that if you can. It shows good faith, and it keeps the rest of the account in order.

Photograph or screenshot the bill before you send anything. Pages and web portals change.

Keep a thread

Save the reply, the date, and the name of anyone you speak to. If you have to phone, ask for a reference number and write it down. Short notes now save a long muddle later.

If nothing happens

No answer in about two weeks? Reply to the same message and ask what their complaints process is. Many companies have a formal one, and many countries have an independent body you can go to if the company can't resolve it. Look up the one for your country if you get that far. For large or complicated disputes, a free advice service in your country is a better guide than a printed sheet.

What counts as done

Done is not a corrected bill. Done is one message sent and one chase date in your phone. You can stop there. The work now sits with them, and your job is to look again on the chase date.

If you can't face writing, a web form or chat works, with the same words. Paste, send, screenshot.
