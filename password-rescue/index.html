Locked out of an account? A ten-minute way to get back in without a spiral

What to do when you are locked out: where to start, how to reset safely, what to tell support if the email never arrives and how to avoid a repeat.

You are staring at a login screen. You have tried three passwords. The fourth might lock you out for an hour. You need into the account today, and you know you will be doing this again in a month.

Being locked out feels worse than it is, because it comes with a small shot of panic and a long list of things that now seem to be going wrong. It is almost always fixable, and it usually takes less time than the dread suggests.

Stop guessing

The first rule is to stop typing passwords. Too many wrong tries can lock an account for longer, and a guess from half-memory rarely works anyway. Use the reset instead. It exists for exactly this.

Ten minutes, in order

Set a timer for ten minutes.

  1. Name the account. Which site or app, and the exact name on it.
  2. List the email or phone it probably uses. Try your main ones.
  3. Check you can still reach that email or phone. If not, that is the real problem, and you fix it first.
  4. Find where your second step lives. If the account uses an authenticator app or a text code, check where that phone is.
  5. Do the reset. Go to the real website or app, type the address in yourself, choose "Forgot password", and follow it. Check spam. Wait five minutes.
  6. Make a new password. A long one, like four unrelated words. Save it in a password manager straight away.

Start with your email

If you have several accounts locked, do the email first. Almost every other reset sends its link to your email. If you lose your email, every other reset breaks. So fix that one, secure it, and the others get much easier.

If the reset email never comes

Check spam and any other folders. Check you used the right address. If it still does not come, contact support using the official site or app:

"Hi, I can't get into my account. I'm no longer receiving the reset email. The account email is [email]. Can you help me verify my identity and regain access?"

If your email or phone has changed:

"Hi, my old [email or phone] is no longer available. Please tell me how to recover the account with other proof, such as a receipt or ID."

Support teams get these requests daily. You do not need a long explanation.

A safety rule worth keeping

Never give a password or a code to someone who contacts you first. A genuine company will not ask for your full password, ever. If a message, call or link says your account has a problem, close it, then open the app or website yourself and look from there.

Stopping it happening again

Once you are in, do three small things. Change the password if you have not. Turn on a second step if the site offers one, and save the backup codes. And store the new password in a manager, so you only have to remember one phrase.

A password manager is a locked notebook that fills your logins in for you. Your phone or browser probably has one built in. Pick a master password of five or six unrelated words and write it on paper, kept somewhere safe at home. Do not tape it to the screen.

What counts as done

Done is back in the account, with a new password saved in your manager. If you cannot get in today, done is the reset requested or support contacted, with the time and date written down. That counts. Some recoveries take a day or two, and that is normal.
