How to report a repair to your landlord when you keep putting it off

A ten-minute way to report the leak, the broken heater or the mould in writing, with proof, a message you can copy and a reminder so it does not get lost.

The damp patch has been there for three weeks. It has grown a little. You have meant to tell the landlord every single day, and every single day it has felt like a bigger job than it should be.

If that is you, nothing is wrong with you. Reporting a repair looks like one small task, but it hides a lot of little ones. You have to work out who to contact, decide how serious it is, find the words, take the photos, and then face the chance of a slow or grumpy reply. With a tired brain, that is a lot of friction for a leaking tap.

Why it keeps slipping

There are two traps. The first is that the fault is not urgent today, so it never wins against the urgent things. The second is that you want to write the perfect message, so you wait until you have the energy for it. Meanwhile the problem usually gets worse, and a worse problem is harder to report calmly.

The way out is to make the message small and boring. A repair report is not a complaint. It is a note that says what is broken, since when, and when someone can come in. That is all.

Ten minutes, in this order

Set a timer for ten minutes.

  1. Name the fault in one line. "Bathroom ceiling has a damp patch above the shower." Add the room. Skip the backstory.
  2. Write down roughly when you first noticed it. "About three weeks ago" is fine.
  3. Take two photos or a short video. One wide shot to show where it is, one close-up. Your phone stamps the date for you, which matters later.
  4. Decide when someone can come in. Or write "please give me notice first". Either is a reasonable answer.
  5. Pick how you will send it. If your landlord or agent replies to texts, text. If they use a portal or email, use that. Writing is better than ringing because it leaves a record.
  6. Send it. Not later. Send it before you tidy the room, before you get the perfect photo.

The message to copy

"Hi, I'm writing to report a repair needed at [address]. There is [the fault] in the [room], which I first noticed around [when]. I've attached photos. Could you let me know when someone can come to look at it? I'm available [days and times]. Thanks."

If a week goes by with no reply:

"Hi, I'm following up on my message of [date] about [the fault]. It hasn't improved. Could you tell me when it will be looked at? Thanks."

That second message is not rude. It is a reminder with a date on it.

One message per fault

If three things are broken, send three short messages. Long messages with a list of everything tend to get a single vague reply, or none. Short ones get answered, and they make it easy to see which fault was fixed and which was not.

When it is not a wait-and-see problem

Gas smells, exposed wiring, a ceiling coming down, a flood or no heating in freezing weather can be safety issues. Use your local emergency number or the emergency line for the gas, power or water company first, then tell your landlord. Do not wait for a reply before keeping yourself safe.

What counts as done

Done is not "the fault is fixed". Done is "the message is sent". Once it is sent, do two small things: put a reminder in your phone for a week from now, and move the photos into an album called REPAIRS, so you can find them when you need them. Keep every reply, and screenshot texts. If the fault is still there in a week, the follow-up is already written above. Rules about repairs and who pays differ from place to place, so if you are unsure what your landlord has to do, a free tenant advice service in your country can tell you.
