How to catch up on your pet's admin when you've put it all off

Your pet doesn't know that the vaccination is overdue, that you can't find the paperwork, or that you haven't checked the microchip details since you moved. You do, though. It sits at the back of your mind like a smoke alarm with a low battery, quiet but not gone.

Pet admin is a pile of small jobs with no urgency until something goes wrong. That's exactly the kind of work that gets lost when you're tired. A week, with one job a day, makes it manageable. This isn't veterinary advice. Anything about your pet's health belongs with your vet. This is about the paperwork, the phone calls and the plan.

The week, one job a day

Each job takes twenty minutes at most. A voicemail left counts as a call made. Miss a day and do it the next. A nine-day week is fine.

  1. Monday: find the records. Vaccination card, adoption papers, vet receipts, insurance emails. Gather them into one folder, and write down what's missing.
  2. Tuesday: ring the vet. Ask what's due, what's overdue, and book the next appointment. Put the date in your phone while you're still on the line.
  3. Wednesday: check the microchip details. Find the chip number and which database holds it, then confirm your name, phone number and address are current. Check the tag on the collar too.
  4. Thursday: look at insurance. Find out whether you have a policy, what it covers and when it renews. If you don't have one, write down how you'd cover a surprise vet bill.
  5. Friday: sort food and supplies. Work out how long a bag lasts, set a reorder reminder ten days before it runs out, and keep one spare day of food somewhere findable.
  6. Saturday: write the emergency page. Your vet's number, the nearest out-of-hours clinic, your pet's details and a person who could help.
  7. Sunday: write the one page. Everything from the week on one sheet, with every date in your phone.

The words to say

For the vet call:

"Hi, I'd like to check what [pet] is due for, like vaccinations or check-ups. Could you let me know what's overdue and book the next appointment?"

For the microchip database, or your vet if they hold the details:

"Hi, I'd like to check the contact details held for my pet's microchip. The number is [number]. Could you confirm what you have and update my phone number?"

For insurance:

"Hi, could you tell me my renewal date and what my policy covers? Could you also confirm the excess?"

And for the friend you're putting on the emergency page:

"Would you be okay being the person on my emergency page for [pet]? It would just mean I could ring you if something happened."

If you're worried about your pet's health at any point, say so at the start of the vet call. Don't wait for the admin to be done first. A vet can tell you whether something needs seeing soon.

Why the microchip day matters most

If your pet is ever lost, the chip is only useful if the details linked to it are right. People move, change numbers and forget to update them. It's a ten-minute fix that matters far more than it looks. While you're at it, check the tag on the collar. The first person to find a lost pet will usually see the tag before anything else.

What counts as done

Done on Monday is one folder. Done on Tuesday is a call or a message and a date in your phone. Done by Sunday is one page you can hand to someone else, and reminders in your phone for the next appointment, the insurance renewal and the food reorder. You don't have to have solved everything. You've replaced a vague worry with a short list, and that's much easier to carry.
