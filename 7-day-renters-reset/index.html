You've been meaning to "sort the flat stuff out" for about a year. The dripping tap, the photos you never took when you moved in, the deposit that's somewhere, the tenancy end date you'd have to dig through emails to find. None of it is hard. All of it is still not done.

That isn't laziness. Renting admin is a pile of small, vague tasks with no deadline and a slightly stressful person at the other end of each one. Your brain quite reasonably files that under "later". The fix is to make each task small, named, and over in twenty minutes.

Why this is harder than it looks

Every one of these jobs has a hidden first step. Before you can report the tap you have to find the agent's email. Before you can check the deposit you have to work out which scheme it's in. Before you can plan a move-out you need dates you don't have. Each hidden step is another reason to close the tab.

There's also a power thing. Messaging a landlord feels like asking for a favour, even when you're asking for something you're entitled to. Having the words ready in advance takes most of that sting out.

The week, one job a day

Nothing here takes more than twenty minutes. Miss a day and you do it the next one; the week can be nine days long.

  1. Monday, photograph everything. Walk through every room with your phone. Walls, floors, appliances, inside the oven, under the sink, the mouldy bit of grout. Date-stamped photos are the single best thing you can own as a tenant.
  2. Tuesday, report the broken thing. Pick the one repair you've been living with. Send one short message, in writing, with a photo. Writing matters because it creates a record.
  3. Wednesday, the contacts page. One page with the landlord or agent's name, email, phone, the emergency repairs number and your tenancy reference. Stick it on the fridge or save it to your phone.
  4. Thursday, where's the deposit? Find out which protection scheme holds it, and check the amount matches what you paid. If you can't find a certificate, ask for one.
  5. Friday, know your dates. When the fixed term ends, how much notice you must give, how much notice they must give you. Put the key dates in your calendar with a reminder a month before.
  6. Saturday, contents cover. Your landlord's insurance covers the building, not your laptop. Get one quote for contents cover. Buying it is optional; knowing the number is the job.
  7. Sunday, the move-out file. One folder, paper or digital, with the photos, the inventory, the deposit details, the dates and every message you've sent. Future you will hug you for this.

The words to say

For the repair, keep it factual and friendly:

"Hi Sam, the kitchen tap has been dripping steadily for a couple of weeks and it's getting worse. Photo attached. Could you let me know when someone can take a look? Thanks, Jo, Flat 4."

For the deposit question:

"Hi, could you confirm which deposit protection scheme my deposit is held with and send me the certificate or reference? I'd like to have it on file. Thanks."

Neither is confrontational. Both are in writing. That's all you need.

What counts as done

Done is not a perfect flat or a fixed tap. Done is: the photos exist, one repair has been reported in writing, the contacts page exists, you know where the deposit is, the dates are in your calendar, you have a contents quote, and there is one folder with everything in it. If the landlord is slow to reply, that's their job unfinished, not yours.
