How to catch up on work admin when you are weeks behind

A week to clear the timesheet, expenses, old emails and the message to your manager, one job a day, with the exact words to send and a Friday habit.

Your actual work is fine. People are happy with it. What is not fine is the rest: the expense claim from two months ago, the timesheet you keep meaning to fill in, the email that has gone red, the form HR sent. Each one is small. Together they sit on your shoulders like a wet coat.

This is a very common problem, especially for people with ADHD or low energy, because admin is boring, vague and has no natural start. It is also fixable, in a week, without staying late.

Why the pile keeps growing

Admin tasks have no urgency until suddenly they do. They do not feel rewarding. And each one needs a small amount of memory, such as what you did on a Tuesday six weeks ago, which fades fast. Putting them off makes each one harder, which makes you put them off more.

A week of one small job a day breaks the loop. The only trick is to do the oldest and most awkward bit first, in short bursts, with the words ready.

The week

Twenty minutes a day. If you miss one, do it the next day.

  1. Monday: write the real list. Everything overdue, on one page, with who is waiting. Circle the three that matter most.
  2. Tuesday: answer the oldest email. One reply, three sentences, sent.
  3. Wednesday: fill in the timesheet. Rebuild it from your calendar and sent folder. Estimates are fine.
  4. Thursday: claim the money. Gather receipts, photograph them, send the claim.
  5. Friday: ask for the thing you need. A login, a clearer brief, more time. One specific request.
  6. Saturday: reply to your manager. A short honest status note. Schedule it for Monday if sending at the weekend feels odd.
  7. Sunday: set up a Friday reset. Ten minutes, three lines, in your calendar.

Words for the awkward bits

The oldest email:

"Hi [name], sorry for the delay on this. [Answer.] Let me know if you need anything else."

If you need time for a proper answer:

"Thanks for your patience. I need until [day] to give you a proper answer. I will send it by then."

A request that unblocks you:

"Hi [name], I am stuck on [task] because I am missing [thing]. Could you help by [day]? That would let me finish by [date]."

A note to your manager:

"Hi [name], quick update. I have finished [A] and [B]. [C] is taking longer than planned and I will have it to you by [day]. If priorities have changed, tell me."

An honest update is nearly always better received than silence. Telling your manager early reads as being on top of things.

A word about expenses and timesheets

Check how long you have to submit a claim, since some workplaces have time limits. If a receipt is lost, a card statement often does the job; ask finance if that is acceptable. For timesheets, estimate honestly and flag anything you are unsure of rather than leaving gaps. A repeating ten-minute reminder on Friday makes both easier from now on.

What counts as done

Done is not an empty inbox. Done is "the three circled things are dealt with and my manager knows where I am". That alone changes how work feels. The rest of the list can shrink at ten minutes a Friday.

If the workload itself is too much, that is a conversation for your manager or HR. It is not a failing in you.
