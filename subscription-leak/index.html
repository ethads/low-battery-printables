How to cancel forgotten subscriptions when you've been avoiding it

There's money leaving your account every month for things you don't use. A streaming service you tried for one series. An app you downloaded for a holiday. A gym. You half know this, which is exactly why you don't look. Looking means finding out the number.

This isn't carelessness. Subscriptions are designed to be forgotten. The sign-up takes one tap and the cancel takes a hunt through four menus. When your brain already spends its working memory on getting through the day, a quiet monthly charge is the easiest thing in the world to never get round to. People call it the ADHD tax. It's real, and it's refundable.

Why this is harder than it looks

"Just check your subscriptions" sounds like one job. It's actually four, because subscriptions hide in four different places and none of them talk to each other. Some are on your card. Some are on your phone's app store account. Some went through PayPal years ago. Some only show up as an email receipt. If you only check the bank, you'll find about half.

Then there's the cancel step, which many companies make deliberately awkward. A chat window. An "are you sure?" screen. A discount offer designed to make you hesitate. Each one is a small place to give up.

The way through is to look in the four places in order, write everything down as you go, and cancel exactly one thing today.

The fifteen-minute version

Set a timer for fifteen minutes. Open your bank app and nothing else to start.

  1. Bank first. Scroll back through three months of transactions. Anything that repeats on the same date each month is a subscription. Write each one down with the amount.
  2. Phone second. Open your app store account settings and find the subscriptions page. This is where the free trials you forgot to cancel live. Write them down.
  3. PayPal third. If you have an account, look for the automatic payments or pre-approved payments section. This is the one most people have never opened.
  4. Email last. Search your inbox for "your receipt" or "renewal". Anything you don't recognise from the first three lists, add it.
  5. Next to each one, write when you last used it. Be honest. "Can't remember" is an answer, and it's a verdict.
  6. Add up what you'd get back each month if you cancelled everything with "can't remember" next to it. Write that number down. It's usually the best number you'll write all week.
  7. Circle one. The easiest, or the most annoying. Cancel that one now.

Most people who do this find two to five subscriptions they'd forgotten about. You don't have to cancel them all today. One is enough. The list is written now, so the rest can happen on another day.

The words to say

Some cancellations are a button. Others want a chat or an email. Keep it short and don't explain yourself.

To cancel:

"Hi, please cancel my subscription with immediate effect and confirm by email that no further payments will be taken. Thanks."

When they offer a discount to stay:

"Thanks, but I'd like to cancel. Please go ahead and confirm it's done."

You don't owe them a reason. "I'd like to cancel" is a complete sentence, and saying it twice is fine.

What counts as done

Done is one cancellation, confirmed. Not the whole list, not a new budget, not a spreadsheet. If you found five and cancelled one, that's money coming back every single month from now on, without you doing anything else. The other four are written down and they aren't going anywhere. Cancel the circled one. Just that one. Then close the laptop.
