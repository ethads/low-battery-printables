How to tackle a mountain of laundry when you can't face it

One load at a time: a short way to sort a huge laundry pile, with a wash plan, a no-fold rule and an alarm so the wash doesn't sit damp.

The pile started as one chair. Then it was the end of the bed. Now it's a heap on the floor, and somewhere in it are clean things and dirty things and a few things you can't classify. You can't wear any of it with confidence, and you don't want to deal with it.

Laundry is a classic low-battery job because it isn't one task. It's about six: gather, sort, load, wait, move it, dry it, put it away. Each step is small, but the gap between them is where it falls over. The wash finishes while you're asleep, the load goes damp, and you rewash it. Quite reasonably, you give up.

Shrink the job to one load

You are not doing the mountain. You are doing one load. The mountain gets smaller every week you start, and the first load is the hardest because it makes the next one possible.

Set a timer for four minutes and make four heaps on the floor:

  1. Whites and lights. T-shirts, underwear, bedding, towels.
  2. Darks. Jeans, black tops, socks. These usually wash cold.
  3. Delicates. Anything that says hand wash, wool or silk. Leave until last.
  4. Skip for now. Dry clean only items, and clean things that ended up in the pile by mistake.

If clean and dirty are mixed together, use the sniff test. Unsure means dirty. No judgement.

Pick the load you need first

Don't start with the biggest heap. Start with the one that includes what you'll want to wear tomorrow. If you're out of clean underwear and socks, that's your first load, even if it's small. Half a load is fine. A full one is not a rule.

Write a quick plan on paper: what's in each load, what temperature, when it goes on, when it's out. The plan matters because the biggest reason laundry stalls isn't loading the machine. It's forgetting there's a wet load inside it.

Set the alarm for the end

Put an alarm on for when the wash ends. Name it something that tells you what to do, like "Move the wash". When it rings, you have a choice: hang it up or put it in the dryer. Don't aim for neat. Hanging it on the airer and leaving it counts as done. If the washing sits for more than a day, a quick rinse cycle usually fixes the smell.

If you can't face the whole thing, there's always the small version:

"Wash on, alarm set, kettle on. That's the day."

The rest of the pile

Folding is optional. Rolling things and putting them in a drawer is folding for tired people. Hang up the things that crease, roll the rest, and put them away in the first place that comes to hand.

No clean socks and no energy? Wash a few pairs by hand tonight. You don't need a full load for socks.

If the pile genuinely won't fit into your week, a laundrette or a wash-and-fold service can take one big load off you. That's not cheating. It's using money to buy back energy, which is exactly what it's for.

What counts as done

On the day, done is one load in the machine and an alarm set. That's the one thing. If it also came out and got hung up, wonderful. If it came out and sat in a basket for a day, still fine. The next load happens when you have the energy, and the pile gets a little less frightening each time.

A good rule: when the pile is down to a size you can see over, you've won. You do not have to reach zero. Almost nobody lives at zero.
