How to reply to the messages you've been ignoring for weeks

Two lines is a full reply. A short way to answer the messages you have been avoiding, with templates for a friend, for work and for saying no.

There's a message you've been meaning to reply to. Then there were three. Now there's a pile, and the oldest one is from a month ago, and every time you open the app your eye lands on it and you close the app again.

This isn't rudeness. It's a loop. The longer the gap, the more you feel the reply needs to explain the gap, and the more it needs to explain, the harder it is to write, so the gap grows. ADHD brains are especially good at this loop because the message stops being "say hi to Sam" and becomes "deal with the Sam situation", which is a much bigger thing and much easier to avoid.

Why this is harder than it looks

The hard part is not the reply. You could type two lines in ten seconds. The hard part is the apology you think has to go in front of it. You picture a paragraph about how busy things have been and how sorry you are and how you're not usually like this. That paragraph is exhausting to write and, honestly, nobody wants to read it.

Here's the thing a month of silence does: it stops being awkward. A month-old message isn't awkward any more. It's just a message. The person on the other end doesn't want the essay. They want to hear from you, and two lines is a full reply.

The other hidden difficulty is that the pile is mixed. Some messages are from people who'll just be glad to hear from you. Some need a yes or no. Some need you to do a thing first. Some are dead and can be archived. Treating them all as one job is what makes the pile feel heavy.

The fifteen-minute version

Set a timer. Open the app. You're sorting first, replying second.

  1. Sort every message into one of four boxes. Someone who'll be glad. Needs a yes or no. Needs a thing. Archive. Be quick. First instinct is right.
  2. Archive the archive pile now. Old group chat noise, newsletters, things that answered themselves. Gone. The pile just got smaller without you writing a word.
  3. Make a reply list. Who, where (text, email, the app), and what they actually need from you. One line each. This turns a feeling into a list, and lists are much easier to look at.
  4. Circle the one you've been thinking about most. Not the oldest, not the most urgent. The one that's been taking up the most space in your head. That one goes first, because sending it feels the best.
  5. Write two lines. Use a template if you need one. Don't explain the gap. Send it.
  6. If there's time, do one more. If not, stop. The list is written.

The words to say

The rule is two lines and no explanation. Here are some that work.

To a friend who'll be glad:

"Hey, sorry I went quiet. I've been thinking about you. How are things?"

To work or admin:

"Hi, apologies for the slow reply. Yes, that works for me. Let me know if you need anything else."

Saying no:

"Thanks so much for asking. I can't make it this time, but I hope it goes well."

When you don't have the thing yet:

"Hi, I haven't got this to you yet, sorry. I'll have it to you by Friday."

Notice none of them explain. "I went quiet" is enough. The other person fills in the rest, and they fill it in kindly.

What counts as done

Done is one message sent. Not the pile cleared. Not every relationship repaired. One reply, to the person you've been thinking about most, and then you close the app and let it land. The rest of the list is written and it'll still be there tomorrow, and each reply gets easier because you've proved to yourself that two lines works. Send the circled one. Then close the app.
