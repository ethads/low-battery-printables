How to ask for a payment plan when you can't pay the whole bill at once

There's a letter on the side. Or an email you haven't opened, because you know roughly what it says. The amount is more than you have right now, and every day it sits there the dread gets bigger than the bill.

Here is something that helps, and it's true: companies would almost always rather be paid slowly than not paid at all. A payment plan is a routine request. The person who takes your call has set up hundreds of them, and a calm, specific ask is one of the easier calls of their day.

Contact them early

If the bill isn't overdue yet, ring before it is. If it is already late, today is still the best day, because it rarely gets easier by waiting. You're not asking for forgiveness. You're proposing a way to pay.

Find your number first (about eight minutes)

The scary part is not knowing what to offer. So work it out on paper before you call.

  1. What you owe. The total, who it's to, and the reference number from the letter or bill.
  2. What comes in. Roughly what lands in your account each month. No need to be exact.
  3. What must go out. Rent, food, heating, transport, essentials. Do this before extras.
  4. What you can offer. Comes in, minus must go out. Offer a little less than the result. That is your number.

A small plan you can keep beats a big one you'll break. If your number is small, say it anyway. Ten is a number.

If you have more than one debt, list them and start with whichever has the earliest deadline. One call is a full day's work.

The words to say

Read these aloud, or type them in a chat or email.

Opening:

"Hi, I'm calling about account [number]. I can't pay the full amount right now. I'd like to set up a payment plan. I can manage [amount] a month."

If they ask why:

"My circumstances have changed. I'd like to keep this on track."

That is a complete answer. You don't owe anyone the whole story.

Before you hang up:

"Can you confirm the plan by email or letter, and confirm nothing else will be added while I keep to it?"

Whether they can promise that depends on the company, so ask, and listen to what they say rather than assuming. If you're unsure about any terms, you can say "I'd like to think about that, can I call you back?" You are allowed to.

Write down the name of the person, the date, the amount, the first payment date and what was agreed.

If you'd rather not phone

Many companies take plans by web chat, email or an online form. The words are the same. Put "payment plan request" in the subject line so it reaches the right team.

If the list is long

When several debts pile up, or you feel overwhelmed, free and confidential money-advice services exist in many countries. Search "free debt advice" plus your country. They can talk through your options and sometimes speak to the company for you. It is not a sign of failure to ring one. It is a very sensible thing to do.

What counts as done

Done is not being debt-free. Done is "I rang, I asked, and I wrote down what they said". If it goes to voicemail, leave your name, the reference and "I'd like to set up a payment plan, please call me back", then mark it tried. That is a real call made.

Once the plan is agreed, set up the payment to leave the day after payday, if you can. Then it happens without you, and you don't have to rely on remembering.

Looking after the next bit

Keep the confirmation somewhere you'll find it. If your situation changes, ring them again before a payment is missed, not after. That's usually the thing that goes best.
