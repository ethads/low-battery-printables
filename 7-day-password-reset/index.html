Somewhere in your life there is a list of accounts you cannot quite get into. You use the same password for most things, with a number on the end that changes. You know it is not great. You have known for years. Every time something locks you out, you promise yourself you will fix the whole system, and then the moment passes.

That is not carelessness. Fixing your accounts looks like one big project and behaves like forty small ones. Each login is a separate chore with its own reset flow, its own rules and its own way of making you feel silly. The sensible response is to avoid the whole lot.

Why a week works better than a weekend

The usual advice is to block out an afternoon and change everything. For a brain with limited energy, that fails by hour one. You get stuck on an account you cannot recover, you lose the thread, and you end up with three half-changed passwords and a worse system than before.

Spread across a week, each day only asks for one small, safe job. You never change everything. You change the things that matter most, in the order that makes the rest easier.

The week, one job a day

Nothing takes longer than twenty minutes. Miss a day and do it the next one. A nine-day week is fine.

  1. Monday: secure the email. Your main email is the key to every other account, because resets go there. Check it opens, check the recovery phone and email are yours, and give it a long, new password.
  2. Tuesday: choose a password manager. Install one on your phone and computer. Create one master password of five or six unrelated words and write it on paper kept safely at home.
  3. Wednesday: move the money accounts. Bank, cards, payment apps. New passwords, made by the manager.
  4. Thursday: add a second step. Turn on two-step verification for the email and the bank, and save the backup codes.
  5. Friday: retire the repeats. Replace three reused passwords, starting with shops that hold a saved card.
  6. Saturday: update the recovery options. Make sure the phone number and email behind your key accounts are current.
  7. Sunday: write the one page. Where the manager is, where the paper is, which email is the main one. No passwords on the page.

The words to say

Most days you will be typing, not talking. But when a reset fails, these help. For a locked account:

"Hi, I can't access my account. I've tried the reset link and it hasn't worked. Can you help me recover it with other proof of identity?"

For a phone number that has changed:

"Hi, my phone number has changed and I can't receive codes on the old one. Can you help me update it and verify my identity another way?"

Always use the real app or the website address you type yourself. If a message or call says your account has a problem and asks for a code or password, do not give it. Close it and look from the official app.

What not to do

Do not write passwords on this week's pages or in a note on your phone. Do not turn on a second step until you have saved the backup codes. Do not try to change everything in one go. The week has only one aim: the accounts that matter are safer, and you have a way back in.

What counts as done

Done on Monday is an email you can open, with correct recovery details. Done on Wednesday is one money account with a new password in the manager. By Sunday, done is a short page in your own handwriting that says where everything is, so that when you are next locked out, the way back is written down. Every three months, spend ten minutes running the manager's weak password check and fixing one.
