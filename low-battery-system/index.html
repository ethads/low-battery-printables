There's a month of life admin sitting in a pile, and the thought of a whole Saturday spent on it makes you want to lie down. So the Saturday never comes. The pile waits. Every week it gets a little heavier and a little easier to walk past.

That isn't laziness. The admin day is a plan that needs a full tank of energy, a clear head and nothing else going on, and those three things almost never land on the same day. Planning around them is planning to fail, then feeling bad about it.

Why this is harder than it looks

A backlog isn't one task. It's seven or eight different kinds of dread stacked together: the letters you haven't opened, the balance you haven't checked, the appointment you haven't booked, the reply you owe. Each one needs its own bit of nerve. Trying to find all that nerve on one day is why the admin day fails. You get through one or two jobs, hit a wall, and the rest roll over to next weekend, slightly scarier.

Spread across a week, each job only needs the nerve for itself. Twenty minutes, one thing, then you're done until tomorrow.

The week, one job a day

Nothing here takes more than twenty minutes. Miss a day and you do it the next day. The week can be nine days long and still count.

  1. Monday: open the scary mail. Open everything, fix nothing, and pull out the one letter that actually matters. Put the rest in a pile for later.
  2. Tuesday: look at the bank. Open the app, read the balance, write the number down, close the app. That's the whole job.
  3. Wednesday: find the subscription leaks. Scan the last statement for anything recurring you'd forgotten about. Cancel one.
  4. Thursday: book the doctor. One phone call with the words ready before you dial.
  5. Friday: answer the messages. The three replies you've been avoiding, short and without the explanation you think you owe.
  6. Saturday: start the form. Gather the numbers, answer five questions, save. Not finish, start.
  7. Sunday: send the parcel back. Print the label, bag it, drop it off before the return window shuts.

At the end there's one more page: what came back (money, appointments, replies), and what to change so the pile never builds to this size again.

The words to say

Most of these jobs stall at the point where you have to talk to someone. Having the words written down takes that away. For the doctor:

"Hi, I'd like to book an appointment with a GP. It isn't urgent. What's the earliest you have, and is there a cancellation list I could go on?"

For a reply you've left far too long:

"Sorry for the slow reply. Yes to Thursday, and thanks for your patience."

For the subscription you want gone:

"Hi, I'd like to cancel my subscription please. Can you confirm by email once it's done?"

None of these need a reason. The person on the other end hears them all day.

What counts as done

Done is seven small jobs started, not a cleared life. The mail opened and the one letter found. The balance written down. One leak plugged. The appointment booked. Three replies sent. Five questions saved. One parcel gone. If a day slipped, it moved to the next one and still got done. That's a week of admin, and it's more than most people manage in a month.
