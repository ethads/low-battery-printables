Somewhere in the bottom of the school bag is a crumpled letter. Somewhere in your phone are three apps and two email threads, each with something you were supposed to do. There is a trip to pay for, a form to sign, a teacher who asked to chat. You meant to deal with it all. You are just so tired.

School admin is relentless and scattered across paper, email, apps and word of mouth. It is no wonder parents with ADHD, or just not much battery, feel like the only ones who cannot keep up. You are not. Here is a calm way through.

Why it feels like too much

Each item is tiny. The trouble is the volume, the different places, and the fact that each has a small deadline that is easy to miss. Missing one makes the next feel worse, and the guilt grows.

The fix is not a perfect system. It is one small job a day for a week, so the pile shrinks and a simple habit takes its place.

The week

Twenty minutes a day or less.

  1. Monday: gather everything. Empty bags, drawers and coat pockets into one box. Open the school email and app. Four loose piles: sign, pay, reply, info.
  2. Tuesday: sign and return forms. Soonest first. Straight into the bag or the app.
  3. Wednesday: pay what is owed. Or ask about instalments or help if money is tight.
  4. Thursday: answer the teacher. One short message.
  5. Friday: fix the calendar. Every school date for the next month in one place, with an alert the night before.
  6. Saturday: make a bag spot. A hook or basket by the door where all school things land.
  7. Sunday: set a weekly routine. A ten-minute school check on the same day each week.

Words to send

If you missed a form:

"Hi, I am sorry this form is late. I am sending it now. Is it still okay to accept it?"

If you lost a letter:

"Hi, I think I missed the letter about [thing]. Could you resend it or tell me what it says?"

If money is tight:

"Hi, I would like to ask whether we could pay for [trip or club] in instalments, or whether there is any support available. I would appreciate a confidential word."

Replying to a teacher:

"Hi [name], thank you for your message and sorry for the delay. [Answer.] Please let me know if there is anything else you need."

Schools have heard all of these. Many have quiet ways to help with costs, and they would rather you asked than your child missed out.

Small things that help a lot

  • The night-before alert. Mornings are for getting out of the door. Put reminders the evening before anything that needs bringing.
  • One home for school things. The basket by the door does more than any amount of remembering.
  • Let your child help. Children stick to systems they helped choose, such as picking the basket or drawing the label.
  • Share the load. If another adult is around, give them one of the weekly jobs.

What counts as done

Done is not "I am a perfectly organised school parent". Done is "the forms are signed, what is due is paid, the teacher has a reply and the dates are in one calendar". That is a lot. The weekly ten minutes keeps it that way, and it takes less effort than the dread did.
