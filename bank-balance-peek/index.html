How to check your bank balance when you're too scared to look

A five-minute way to look at your bank account when you have been avoiding it, with no budget, no spiral, and scripts for moving a payment date.

You haven't opened your banking app in a while. Not because you forgot. Because you have a feeling about what's in there, and the feeling is bad enough that not knowing seems kinder. So the app stays closed and the feeling grows.

This isn't irresponsibility. It's a very normal response to a thing that feels like a verdict. Money anxiety and an ADHD brain are a bad pairing: the brain avoids what hurts, and the avoidance makes the hurt bigger, and round it goes. The number in the account doesn't change while you're not looking, though. It's already true. Imagining it is always worse than reading it.

Why this is harder than it looks

"Just check your balance" assumes that looking is the whole job. It isn't. Looking is the start of a chain: see the number, work out what's still to come out, work out when payday is, do the subtraction, then sit with the answer. Every link in that chain is a place to spiral. Most people who avoid their bank app aren't avoiding the number. They're avoiding the ten minutes of dread after it.

And most advice makes it worse. "Make a budget." "Track every penny." That's a whole project, and you can't start a project from inside a panic. You need something much smaller.

The goal here is not a budget. It's five minutes, four numbers, and one decision.

The five-minute version

Set a timer for five minutes. Have a pen and a scrap of paper. Open the app.

  1. Write down the balance now. Just the number. Don't react to it yet. Writing it down moves it from your head to the paper, which is where it should be.
  2. Write down what's going out before payday. Rent or mortgage, the phone, the direct debits you know about. Rough is fine. Scroll back a month in the app if you can't remember what's regular.
  3. Write down payday. The date. Count the days.
  4. Work out the gap. Balance minus what's going out. That's the only sum on the page. It's either plus or minus.
  5. Next to each outgoing, ask "can it move?" Most payment dates can be shifted with one phone call or a few taps. Most people don't know this. If the gap is minus, this column is where the answer is.
  6. Pick a "next peek" date. The day after payday is good. Write it down and put it in your phone. A five-minute look every payday is what stops the fear building up again.

When the timer goes, stop. You know the number now, and knowing is most of the relief.

The words to say

If the gap is minus, you make one call. Not five. One. The scripts are short because the people on the other end do this all day.

Moving a payment date:

"Hi, I'd like to move my payment date to the 2nd of the month so it lands after I get paid. Can you do that from the next payment onwards?"

Asking for a short plan when you'll be short:

"Hi, I'm going to be a bit short this month. Could we set up a short arrangement to spread this payment over the next two months?"

Asking for a fee to be waived:

"Hi, I've been charged a fee on my account. I've not had one before and I'd be grateful if you could waive it as a one-off."

They say yes to that last one more often than you'd think. You lose nothing by asking.

What counts as done

Done is knowing the number and making one decision about it. If the gap is minus, you make the one call that moves the biggest thing, and that's today's whole job. If the gap is plus, you close the app and let yourself feel the relief, because you earned it by looking. Either way, you don't build a budget, you don't open a spreadsheet, you don't fix your whole financial life tonight. If the gap is minus, make the one call. If it's plus, close the app. Either way, done.
