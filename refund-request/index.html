How to ask for a refund politely when the thing you bought arrived wrong

Imagine the box is in the hallway. The thing inside is cracked, or the wrong size, or not what the page described. You know you should send it back. You also know that means finding the order, working out the policy, writing something polite and waiting for a reply you'll worry about.

So it sits there. A week passes. The window for returns might be closing, and now the box feels like a small, guilty monument.

Why it stalls

Asking for a refund feels like asking a favour. It isn't. You paid for something, it wasn't right, and a company has a process for exactly this. The difficulty is not the ask. It is the pile of small decisions around it: where's the receipt, what do I say, what do I want them to do.

Let's do those decisions first, so the message only has to be assembled.

Step one: gather four facts

Write each in a line.

  • What and where. What you bought, from whom, and the order or booking number.
  • When and how much. The date you paid, the amount, and how you paid.
  • What went wrong. One or two plain sentences. "Arrived cracked." "Not as described." "Never arrived."
  • What you want. A full refund, a partial refund, a replacement or credit. Pick one. Vague asks get vague answers.

Step two: collect proof

Take photos now, before you repack or throw anything away. You want a photo of the problem, a photo of the packaging if it's damaged, and the receipt or the order email. Save them in one folder.

A clear photo plus the order number settles most refund requests in one exchange. If the problem is "it never arrived", the tracking page or a screenshot of the status does the same job.

Step three: send the message

Here is a version to copy:

"Hello, I'm writing about order [number], placed on [date] for [amount]. [One sentence on what went wrong.] I'd like a [full refund / replacement]. I've attached [photo / receipt]. Could you let me know what happens next, and roughly how long it will take?"

That's it. It is calm, specific and doesn't apologise for existing. You don't need to say sorry for bothering them, and you don't need to explain your whole week.

If they go quiet or say no

Wait a few days, then reply on the same thread so the history stays together:

"Following up on my request below. Could you confirm whether this refund will be approved?"

If they refuse and you still think it's fair, ask them what the complaints process is. If you paid by card or through a payment service, it is also worth asking that provider what protection applies to a faulty or undelivered purchase. Those rules differ by country and by card, so check yours rather than assuming.

What counts as done

Done is not "the money is back". Done is "the message is sent". Attach the photo, paste the text, press send, and put a reminder in your phone for a week later. When the reminder goes off, you check for a reply. If there is one, great. If not, you send the follow-up.

Keep a note of who you wrote to and when. If it takes more than one try, you'll be glad of the dates.

A thought for next time

Most refund stress comes from waiting. Next time something arrives wrong, take the photo the same day you notice and send the message the same night. Five minutes today saves a week of dread, and the policy deadline never creeps up on you.
