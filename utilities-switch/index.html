How to switch your electricity, gas or internet provider when you can't face comparing prices

A twenty-minute way to find what you pay for power, gas or internet, compare one cheaper deal and switch, or ask your current company for a better price.

You know you are probably paying too much. Most people are. The bill arrives, you wince, and you tell yourself you will look into it at the weekend. A year later you are on the same plan, at a higher price, because the provider quietly moved you there when the first deal ended.

The problem is not that you do not care. Comparing deals asks you to find your bill, understand it, open several tabs, line up different prices, and then trust a stranger with something that keeps your lights on. That is a long job to start when your energy is already low.

What makes it harder than it looks

Bills hide the useful numbers. Your usage, your contract end date and any exit fee are scattered around the page. Comparison sites ask for exactly those numbers, so you end up hunting before you can even begin. There is also a fear that switching means a gap in supply, or a mess if it goes wrong. In most places the new provider handles the whole move, and you keep the same supply, but nobody tells you that.

So the trick is to do the digging first, on paper, then do the comparing in one short burst.

Twenty minutes, in three steps

Set a timer. You are switching one bill today, not all of them. Pick the one that costs most.

Step 1: dig out four facts (5 minutes). From your latest bill or online account, write down who the company is and what the service covers, what you pay per month, when your contract ends and whether there is an exit fee, and your usage. Usage might be kilowatt hours, gigabytes or the speed of your connection. Your comparison site will ask for it.

Step 2: compare (8 minutes). Use a comparison site that your country's regulator or consumer body recommends. Enter your usage. Look at three or four results, write them in a small table, and put what you currently pay in the first row.

Step 3: choose (a few minutes). Circle the best row that is clearly cheaper and has no exit fee you would have to pay. If nothing is clearly better, stay where you are and write the date to look again. That is also a result.

The words to say

You can try your current company first. Sometimes they have a better plan for people who ask.

"Hi, I'm looking at moving to another provider because of the price. Is there a better plan available for me, or any loyalty offer?"

If they say no, switch. When you sign up with the new provider:

"Hi, I'd like to sign up to the [plan] I saw on the comparison site. I'm moving over from [current company]. Can you confirm the start date and that there will be no gap in supply?"

Do not cancel the old contract yourself. The new provider normally does it, which avoids a gap. Afterwards, check the final bill from the old company. If it looks wrong, ring and say:

"Hi, I've moved to a new provider from [date]. Can you check my final bill uses the meter reading I gave on [date]?"

Before the switch date

Take a photo of your meter, or write the reading down, on the day the switch happens. It is the best proof you can have if the final bills disagree.

What counts as done

Done is one bill moved, or one call made asking for a better rate. Not all of them. Write down the saving per month, because seeing that number is the reward, and put the switch date in your phone. If you got as far as choosing a deal but not signing up, circle it and set a reminder for tomorrow. Compare prices once a year and you will keep most of the benefit.
